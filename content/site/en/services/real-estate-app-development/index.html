The problem a real estate app solves

Property listing portals bring enquiries, but they place your listing among hundreds of competing ones, and the enquiry arrives as a call or message recorded nowhere. A week later the sales manager does not know how many prospects called, which of them viewed, or why they did not buy.

An app in your company’s name shows only your properties and turns each sign of interest into a record: who asked, about which unit, when they viewed and what the next step is. After the sale or lease its longer role begins: the off-plan buyer follows payments and construction, the tenant pays and reports a fault, the owner sees a statement. Those services are what make people keep the app.

An app, or a property website?

Someone looking for a flat starts on Google and on listing portals, and will not download the app of an office they have never heard of. So a property website built for search is the acquisition channel and cannot be skipped. If your goal is only to show properties and collect enquiries, a website is enough and it ranks in search.

An app earns its cost in two cases: a large, fast-changing inventory that seekers and investors follow daily and want instant alerts on, or an ongoing relationship after the contract with buyers, owners and tenants. In the second case the app is a service tool, not a marketing tool, and that is what justifies it for developers and property managers.

  • Showing listings and collecting enquiries only: start with a website.
  • Off-plan buyers, or tenants and owners: the app is how you serve them.
  • The website and the app read from one listings database.

What the first version should contain

For a broker or developer focused on sales: listings with photos and a map, search and filters, favourites and alerts, viewing bookings, contact with the agent, and a dashboard where listings are entered and enquiries are assigned.

For a property management company the priority is different: tenant and owner sign-in, payments and reminders, maintenance requests and documents. We advise choosing one of the two paths for the first version, not both, because combining them doubles the timeline and delays learning what people actually use.

How the app connects to the company’s systems

A listing that was sold but is still showing costs the app its credibility. So the app reads unit status from your property management or sales system where it offers an interface, or listings are managed from the app’s own dashboard if you have no system.

Enquiries and viewing appointments flow into the CRM and are assigned to an agent, payments go through a licensed payment gateway and are posted to accounting, and invoices are issued by an accounting system that meets e-invoicing requirements. Lease and sale contracts stay in their official channels; the app shows the client a copy and the dates of their commitments.

  • Property management system: units, availability and prices.
  • CRM: enquiries, viewings and follow-up.
  • A licensed payment gateway and accounting.
  • Map services, billed by usage.

What to prepare before we start

A property app is only as good as its data. A listing without good photos, a floor plan and an accurate location cannot be rescued by any design. If property advertising in your line of business requires a licence or an advertisement number, confirm the requirements with the regulator, and we add the fields needed to display it on every listing.

  • The list of properties and units with consistent fields: type, area, price and status.
  • Photos, floor plans and accurate map locations.
  • User roles: prospect, buyer, owner, tenant, broker, manager.
  • Your current property or sales system and its provider.
  • A written reservation, deposit and refund policy.

Common mistakes with real estate apps

The first is building an open marketplace for every advertiser when what was needed was an app for one company. An open marketplace is a different project with a different budget and marketing plan, and it has to attract advertisers and seekers at the same time. Decide which of the two you want before asking for quotes.

The second is neglecting whoever enters the data. If adding a property takes ten minutes, staff will not keep listings current. We design listing entry from a phone, with photos and location, in two minutes. The third is forgetting the monthly cost of maps, a running cost that belongs in the budget from the start.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a real estate app cost?

A real estate app with us starts from EGP 180,000. Yours depends on the number of roles and integrations, and we send a written quote within 24 hours.

How long does a real estate app take?

From 10 to 16 weeks for the first release. A listings and viewings app for one company sits near the lower end; adding owner and tenant services, payments and integration with your systems moves it towards the upper end. The delivery date is written into the contract and we keep it, and we deliver in stages you try with your real property data.

Can you build an app like the well-known property listing apps?

We build platforms with a similar idea, with your own design and code. But separate two projects: an app for your company’s properties, and an open marketplace where anyone advertises. The second needs listing moderation, advertiser verification and a marketing budget to attract both sides. We help you decide which fits your goal before the scope is written.

Can the app connect to our property management system or CRM?

Yes, where the system offers an interface. Units, their status and prices are read from your system, and enquiries, appointments and payments are written to it. We review the system before contracting and write into the scope which data moves in each direction. If you have no system, the app’s dashboard manages listings and clients and can be extended later.

Can a client reserve a unit and pay the deposit in the app?

Yes. The reservation amount is paid through a licensed payment gateway by mada, card or Apple Pay, the unit is held for a period you set, a receipt is issued and sales are notified. Your company sets the reservation and refund policy, and the client sees it before paying. Selling off-plan units is a regulated activity, so confirm its requirements with the regulator.

Does the app serve tenants and owners?

Yes, and that is what keeps the app in use after the contract. The tenant sees and pays instalments and reports and tracks maintenance; the owner sees occupancy and a statement. Each role has its own screens and permissions, so one owner never sees another’s data. These services rely on the data in your property management system.

What about virtual tours and project plans?

We support virtual tours, video and interactive floor plans that show available and reserved units. Filming and producing the tours themselves is done by specialist providers, and we display them inside the app. We advise starting with clear photos and plans for every unit before investing in tours.

What are the monthly costs after launch?

Hosting, map services billed by usage, one-time codes and notifications, Apple’s developer account fee, and a maintenance contract if you choose one. They grow with the number of users, listings and photos. We estimate them in the quote, and design search and the map so they do not consume more paid services than necessary.

Does the company own the code and the data?

Yes. The source code, the listings database, client data, store accounts and server are in your company’s name, as the contract states. The base of prospects the app builds is your commercial asset; we neither keep it nor use it. After launch we provide fast technical support, and you can move the project to your own team whenever you wish.
