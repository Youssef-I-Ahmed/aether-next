Off-the-shelf ERP or a custom system? How to decide

Off-the-shelf software works well when your operations are close to standard: buying, selling, stock and accounting without much complexity. You start quickly and the monthly subscription is low at first. The picture changes when your company has its own way of working, such as pricing by dimensions, multi-level approvals, or work orders tied to contracts. That is when paid customisations begin, and your team ends up working around the software instead of the software working for them.

A custom system is built around how you work. You own its code and data outright, and there are no per-user annual licences. The upfront cost is higher than a monthly subscription, but the fair comparison is against three years of licences, customisations and consulting. A practical rule: if more than a third of your daily work happens outside your current system, in Excel and WhatsApp, custom is usually cheaper over the medium term.

There is a third route we often recommend: keep your accounting software if it serves you well, build only what it lacks, such as quotations, contracts and work orders, and connect the two. You pay only for the real gap, and your finance team keeps the tool they know.

Integrating your ERP with ZATCA e-invoicing Phase 2

Phase 2 (the integration phase) requires your system to send invoices directly to Fatoora, the platform run by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA). B2B tax invoices must be cleared before they reach the customer, and simplified B2C invoices must be reported within 24 hours. Every invoice is generated as XML to the published specification and carries a cryptographic stamp, a QR code and a tamper-proof sequence.

Invoicing requirements vary by market and document type. Check the Egyptian Tax Authority or the relevant authority in the market where your business operates.

When we build an ERP, Fatoora integration is part of the system from day one, not an add-on. If you already have a system that works well, we can integrate it on its own without rebuilding it, and tell you upfront which route costs less.

  • Onboarding your invoicing units and obtaining the cryptographic stamp certificate (CSID) from the Fatoora portal in your company’s name
  • Compliance testing in the sandbox first, then moving to production once the test invoices pass
  • Credit and debit notes linked to the original invoice, plus handling and resubmitting rejected invoices
  • Archiving invoices in their electronic format so they can be retrieved for audit

What to prepare before the first meeting

The fastest ERP projects start with a clear picture of how the business runs today, not a long wish list. You don’t need a formal requirements document; just gather what your team actually uses now, and we turn it into a written scope.

Start with the department that hurts most. In most companies that is sales and inventory, or quotations and work orders. That becomes phase one, and everything after it is built on top of the same database.

  • Samples of your current documents: a quotation, invoice, purchase order, work order and customer statement
  • The Excel files your team relies on every day, however messy
  • The number of users, branches and warehouses, and who approves what
  • The software you need to integrate with or migrate from, such as your accounting or POS system
  • The five reports you most want to see every morning

How to compare two ERP quotes

Two quotes, one double the other, may describe two completely different systems. The gap is usually in what was left out: legacy data migration, staff training, ZATCA integration, hosting and post-launch support. Ask each vendor to list included and excluded modules clearly, then compare line items, not totals.

Ask about ownership too. Are the code, the database and the hosting account in your name? Are there annual per-user fees? What happens if you want to change vendor in two years? A system you don’t own ties you to one supplier however much their prices rise.

Finally, tie payments to deliverables. A quote that asks for half the value before anything is delivered puts all the risk on you. Better are clear phases, each with screens you can see working before the next payment.

Common mistakes that derail ERP projects

The first mistake is switching every module on in a single day. The result is a confused team and dirty data. A phased rollout, one module at a time, gives people time to adjust and surfaces errors early, while they are still small.

The second is migrating legacy data as is. Stock balances that don’t reconcile and duplicate customer names will follow you into the new system, and management will lose trust in it within the first week. So we clean and reconcile the data before migration, and run both systems in parallel for a short period when needed.

The third is ignoring the end user. The storekeeper, the accountant and the sales rep are the people who will use the system every day; involving them in screen reviews before launch saves weeks of resistance afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a custom ERP system cost in Egypt?

Cost varies widely with the number of modules, branches and users, the integrations required and the data to migrate. An ERP with us starts from EGP 282,000, and the final price depends on the modules you start with. The accurate way is for us to understand your business in a short meeting, then send a written quote within 24 hours, broken into phases so you know what each module costs and can start with what you need first.

What is the difference between off-the-shelf and custom ERP?

With off-the-shelf ERP you pay a monthly or annual subscription and adapt to the software’s way of working; it suits standard operations that need few changes. A custom ERP is built around your processes, you own its code and data, and there are no per-user licences. Off-the-shelf is cheaper at the start; custom is usually cheaper once customisations and licences pile up, or when you have a workflow packaged software cannot handle without workarounds.

Do you integrate the system with ZATCA e-invoicing Phase 2?

Yes. We build invoice generation as XML with the cryptographic stamp and QR code, and connect it to Fatoora for clearance of B2B invoices and reporting of simplified invoices. We start by onboarding your invoicing units in the compliance sandbox, then move to production once the test invoices pass. We can also integrate your existing system on its own if it serves you well, so you don’t have to replace it before your wave deadline.

Do I own the source code?

Yes. The source code, the database, the hosting accounts and the domain are in your name, and the contract says so before work begins. There are no annual per-user licences, and you can later develop the system with any team you choose without our permission. This is one of the biggest differences between a custom system and a subscription to packaged software, where your data stays tied to the subscription.

How long does an ERP implementation take?

We work in phases. Phase one, usually sales and inventory or quotations and work orders, goes live in 6 to 10 weeks. The complete system with all its modules takes 3 to 6 months, depending on its size and the number of branches. Phasing lets you benefit early and gives your team time to adjust, instead of waiting for one huge system delivered all at once that overwhelms everyone.

I already have accounting software. Do I need to replace it?

Not necessarily. If your accounting software serves you well and supports e-invoicing, we build only what it lacks, such as quotations, contracts, inventory and work orders, and connect it so journal entries and invoices flow across automatically. We recommend replacing it only when it is the cause of double entry and errors, or when it cannot integrate with other systems.

Can you migrate my data from Excel or an old system?

Yes. We migrate customers, suppliers, items, opening balances and open invoices from Excel files or the old system’s database. Before migrating we clean the data, remove duplicates and reconcile balances with you and your accountant, because migrating inaccurate data makes management lose trust in the new system within a week and sends the team back to Excel.

Where is the company data hosted?

We host the system on the cloud provider of your choice, and the data can be hosted in-region on request. We apply daily backups, encryption, permissions and an audit trail, and we follow the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) when handling employee and customer data. The hosting account is in your name, so your data is never held by anyone else.

Does the system work on mobile?

Yes. The system runs in the browser on desktop and mobile, and field screens such as stock counts, receiving and sales-rep orders are designed mobile-first. If your team needs an app-store app, for example for sales reps or technicians, it is built on the same database rather than as a separate system.

Do you work with companies outside Riyadh?

Yes. We serve companies across Egypt and the wider MENA region remotely and work on Egypt time. We hold online meetings to understand your business and review each phase, keep in daily touch on WhatsApp, and you see your project’s progress every week in the client portal, wherever your company is and however many branches it has.

What happens after the system is delivered?

We hand over the system with training for your team on every screen, and provide fast technical support after launch to fix any issue quickly. You can also agree a monthly or annual support and development contract with us to add new modules, or run the system with your own team, because the code and accounts are yours.
