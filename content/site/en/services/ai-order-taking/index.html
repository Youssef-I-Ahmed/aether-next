AI order taking on WhatsApp and the phone for restaurants and shops

Book a consultation Get a quote on WhatsAppRequest a quote

AI order taking is a digital employee that takes the whole order on a restaurant’s or shop’s WhatsApp and phone line. It understands what the customer types or says, picks the items, sizes and extras from the approved menu or catalogue, reads the order back with its total for confirmation, takes the address or collection time, then passes it to the point-of-sale system where the system offers an interface, or to the till. It suits restaurants, cafés and shops that take orders by message and call and whose line is jammed at peak times. Delivery usually takes 3 to 6 weeks, and this kind of project starts with us from EGP 37,500, with the final price set by the flows and connected systems. You can try a live digital employee now on the /try page, and we send a written quote within 24 hours.

Who is this for?

  • Restaurants and cafés whose phone rings at peak time with nobody free to take the order
  • Kitchens, patisseries and bakeries taking orders on WhatsApp by hand
  • Retail shops whose customers order by message and ask about stock and delivery
  • Chains with several branches that want one menu and one way of taking an order
  • Businesses that want a direct ordering channel where the customer data stays with them

What you get

Takes the order in the customer’s words

It understands “two chicken burgers, no onion, and a large fries” as typed or spoken, turns it into items from the menu, and asks for what is missing, such as the size or the drink.

The menu and prices as you approved them

Items, sizes, extras and prices are read from the approved menu or catalogue only. It invents no item, alters no price and grants no unannounced offer.

Reads the order back

Before sending, it repeats the items, the extras, the total and the delivery or collection details, and the order goes nowhere until the customer clearly agrees.

Delivery or collection

It takes the address or location and checks it falls inside the approved delivery area, or fixes the branch and collection time, and gives the estimated time you set.

Passes it to the POS or till

The order reaches the point-of-sale system where the system offers an interface, or a panel the cashier watches, with an alert and a clear order number.

Items that have run out

You switch an item off from a simple panel and it stops being offered on every channel. It suggests the alternative you set instead of taking an order that cannot be made.

Changing and cancelling an order

It takes changes and cancellations within the time you allow before preparation starts. After that it passes the customer to the branch.

A voice version for the shop phone

It answers ordering calls in a natural voice at busy times, takes the order during the call, then sends the customer a written summary.

The order lost at peak time

The peak hour is when most customers order, and it is the very hour when the cashier has no minute to answer the phone or read WhatsApp. A customer who sends an order and waits ten minutes with no reply orders from somewhere else, and you never know they tried.

The digital employee takes every order at the same moment, however many there are, and sends them to the kitchen or the till in order. What stays with the team is preparing, handing over and serving the customer standing in front of them.

How it takes an order without mistakes

A wrong order costs more than a slow one: a wrong item means a returned meal and an annoyed customer. So we build the flow on three safeguards: items come from the menu only, everything missing is asked about, and the whole order is read back before it is sent.

Before launch we test it on real orders from your WhatsApp archive, with items named the way your customers name them and not the way the menu does, with abbreviations and typos. Every everyday name for an item is added as a synonym, so it understands “the family meal” and “the offer” as your regulars mean them.

  • Understanding the order and turning it into items, sizes and extras from the menu
  • Asking for what is missing: size, temperature, drink, notes
  • Reading back the order and total, and waiting for the customer’s agreement
  • Taking the address, or the branch and collection time
  • Sending the order with its number and telling the customer the approved estimated time

From the chat to the kitchen or the till

If your point-of-sale or ordering system offers an interface, the order enters it like any other and appears in the kitchen. If it does not, the order reaches a simple panel the cashier watches, with a sound alert, or the team’s WhatsApp, and the cashier then enters it into the system.

In the first session we tell you which of the two is possible with your system, because the difference is real: the first needs no human hand, and the second needs a person to accept the order. Both are better than orders scattered across chats nobody is following.

For shops: ordering from the catalogue

In a retail shop the question changes from “what’s on the menu” to “is this in stock”. The digital employee answers from the list of products and stock your team keeps up to date, or reads it from the stock system where the system offers an interface. It takes the size, colour and quantity and fixes the order for delivery or collection from the branch.

It does not replace an online store for anyone selling a large catalogue, but it serves the customer who prefers to order by message, which is a common habit. If you have a store, it sends the customer to the product page when that is easier for them.

Payment, allergens and what it does not do

The digital employee neither asks for nor accepts card numbers in the chat. Payment is made the way you approve: on delivery or collection, or through a payment link your own system issues. On allergens it states only what the approved menu says, and passes anyone who mentions a severe allergy to a person, because that answer carries a responsibility an automated reply should not take.

It does not promise a delivery time you have not approved, compensate for a late order or settle a complaint. Complaints go to the branch manager with a summary of the order and the conversation. Offers and “what’s new” messages go only to people who agreed to receive them, as templates Meta has approved.

Frequently asked questions

How much does AI order taking cost?

A digital employee starts with us from EGP 37,500 and rises with a point-of-sale integration, the number of branches and the size of the menu, plus a monthly running cost for hosting, the AI model and follow-up. We send a written quote within 24 hours once we have seen your menu and how you take orders.

Does the order go straight into the till system?

Where the point-of-sale system offers an interface, yes, and the order appears in the kitchen like any other. Where it does not, it reaches a panel or the team’s WhatsApp and the cashier accepts it. We review your system first and tell you plainly which route is available before any commitment.

What if it gets the order wrong?

That is why it reads the whole order back with its total and sends nothing until the customer agrees. If it does not recognise an item it asks, and does not guess. After two attempts it hands over to a person. We test it on real orders from your archive before launch and review the first orders with the team.

Does it take payment from the customer?

It takes no card details in the chat. Payment is made on delivery or collection, or through a payment link your system issues, if it can, which the digital employee sends to the customer. We set the method by what you approve in your business and what your system allows.

What if an item runs out or a price changes?

You switch the item off or change the price from a simple panel, and the reply changes on WhatsApp and the phone at once. If the menu is read from the point-of-sale system where an integration is available, the digital employee follows what the system says. It accepts no order for an item that is switched off.

Does it replace delivery apps?

No, but it gives you a direct ordering channel on your own number where the customer data stays with you. Many businesses run both: the apps to reach new customers, and the direct channel for those who know you and order from you regularly.

Does it suit a shop that is not a restaurant?

Yes, any business that takes orders by message and call: sweets, flowers, perfume, groceries, supplies. It answers on stock from a list you keep up to date or from the stock system where an integration is available, and fixes the order for delivery or collection. For large catalogues we recommend an online store alongside it.

Does it send offers to customers who ordered before?

Only to people who agreed to hear from you, and as templates Meta has approved. We add the consent at the first order and put an easy way to opt out in every message. Sending starts small and rises gradually to protect the number.

Does it take orders by phone?

Yes. The voice version takes the order and reads it back during the call, then sends a written summary. It introduces itself as an automated assistant, and if calls are recorded the caller is told. In very noisy places it may ask the customer to repeat themselves, or pass them to a person.
