  • Companies that want a direct sales or service channel to customers on mobile instead of relying on middlemen.

  • Companies with a stalled or half-finished app that need someone to take it over, finish it or rebuild it.
  • Businesses offering bookings, subscriptions or memberships that want a smooth Arabic mobile experience.

Do you really need an app, or is a responsive website enough?

The first question we ask before any quote: what will the customer or employee do in the app that they cannot do on a good website? An app earns its cost when usage is frequent, such as weekly orders or tracking bookings, or when you need device features like location, camera, notifications and offline work.

If visitors come once or twice a year to read about your services and get in touch, a responsive website or web platform is cheaper, faster to launch and easier to rank on Google. Many companies start with a web platform and add the app once repeat usage is proven, and we recommend that path when it fits.

  • Choose an app if customers will come back weekly or every other day.
  • Choose an app if you need location, the camera or offline use.
  • Start with a website or web platform if your main goal is search visibility and collecting enquiries.

What to prepare before the first meeting

You do not need a full requirements document to start, but five pieces of information save weeks: who the users are and how many types there are, the core journey that must work in the first release, which systems the app must connect to, how you will collect money, and who on your team will approve designs and test.

If there are competitor apps you like or dislike, send us their names; that is the fastest way to show the level you expect. If you have a brand identity, send it over; if not, identity design can be included in the project scope.

  • The list of roles: customer, driver, branch manager, supervisor and so on.
  • The most important journey, from opening the app to completing the transaction.
  • Existing systems: accounting, ERP, inventory, CRM, payment gateway.
  • The person responsible for approvals on your side and their expected turnaround.

How to compare app development quotes

The gap between two app quotes can be large, and it is not just the price, it is what each one covers. A very cheap quote may be a ready-made app recoloured, a web wrapper showing a website inside an app, or it may exclude the admin dashboard, the server and publishing. Ask every provider for a written screen list and a clear scope before you compare.

Ask each provider three questions: are the source code, store accounts and server in my name? How fast is technical support after launch, and what does it cover? How are payments tied to deliverables? Vague answers to these questions are the most common reason app projects stall in this market.

  • Make sure the quote states the number of screens, the roles and the admin dashboard explicitly.
  • Ask about monthly running costs: server, SMS, maps and notifications.
  • Make sure payments are staged and tied to deliverables you can try yourself.

The technology we choose, and why

For most business projects we use a cross-platform framework to build the iOS and Android versions together, with a separate server and APIs and a web admin dashboard. That means one team and one codebase to maintain, and updates that reach both platforms at the same time.

We go fully native per platform only when the project genuinely needs it, such as heavy camera or audio processing or deep integration with system features. We choose where data is hosted based on its sensitivity, with in-region hosting available on request and the Personal Data Protection Law taken into account.

After launch: an app is an ongoing project

An app does not end on release day. iOS and Android update every year, the stores change their rules, and users reveal needs that never came up in planning. So we hand over a dashboard for tracking crashes and usage, and provide fast technical support after launch to fix any issue quickly.

For ongoing care you can stay with us on a clear monthly maintenance contract, or hand the code to your in-house team with full documentation. We recommend budgeting 15 to 20 percent of the build cost each year for maintenance and improvements; that is the realistic figure in the market.

Saudi requirements we build in from the start

An app serving customers in Saudi Arabia needs details global templates miss: prices shown including VAT, a correct tax invoice, the payment methods people actually use such as mada and Apple Pay, number and date formatting in Hijri and Gregorian, and an address tied to the National Address so drivers can find it easily.

In apps that handle personal or financial data we design for the Personal Data Protection Law from the start: clear consent, collecting as little data as possible, precise permissions in the admin dashboard, and a log of who viewed what. If your service requires identity verification, we can integrate with Nafath.

  • Prices and invoices that account for VAT.
  • mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay, Tabby and Tamara.
  • Hijri and Gregorian dates, and a right-to-left Arabic interface.
  • In-region data hosting on request.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to build a mobile app in Egypt?

A business app with us starts from EGP 180,000; the final price depends on the number of screens and roles, online payments, integrations and live tracking. We send a detailed written quote within 24 hours of understanding your requirements. It lists the screens, roles and integrations explicitly, so you can compare it with other quotes on the same basis instead of comparing totals without knowing what is behind them.

How long does it take to build an app?

The first release of a business app usually takes 8 to 16 weeks: one to two weeks for requirements analysis and initial designs, then development in stages you try yourself, then testing and publishing to the stores. The timeline is written into the contract: on-time delivery, guaranteed. What most often extends it is late design approval or scope changes during the build, so we agree on a single approver on your side from the start.

Do I own the code after delivery?

Yes. The source code, App Store and Google Play accounts, server and domain are in your company's name. We add ourselves as developers on your accounts and never hold them, and we hand over the repository and documentation, so you can move to any other team whenever you like. This is a contract clause, not a verbal promise, because withheld accounts are one of the most common complaints from app owners in the market.

What is the difference between Flutter and native development?

Flutter and React Native build the iOS and Android apps from one codebase, which lowers cost and maintenance and ships every update to both platforms together; it is the best fit for most business apps. Separate native development is chosen when the app needs heavy camera or audio processing or advanced system features. We decide the technology during analysis and explain the choice in writing, and we never pick it because it is easiest for us.

Can you build an app like a well-known app I use?

Yes. We build apps with ideas similar to well-known delivery, booking or marketplace apps, but with your own design and code, not a copy. We usually start with a first version focused on the core journey, then add features based on market response instead of building everything at once. That lowers the starting cost and the risk, and gives you real usage data to base phase-two decisions on.

Can the app connect to my accounting system or ERP?

Yes, if your system offers an API or a database that can be accessed securely. We connect orders, customers, inventory and invoices, and ZATCA-compliant e-invoices can be issued automatically from your accounting system. We review your current system during analysis to choose the best integration method, and write it into the scope along with the data flowing in each direction.

What are the monthly costs after launch?

They usually include cloud hosting, OTP and notification messages, map services if used, and Apple's annual developer fee. They vary with the number of users, and we give you an estimate in the quote so they do not surprise you after launch. We design the app so it does not consume paid services unnecessarily, and choose providers that fit your actual usage in the first year.

How do I make sure the project does not stall halfway?

We write the scope and screens into a contract before starting, tie every payment to a deliverable you try yourself, you follow weekly progress in the client portal, and the project manager contacts you daily on WhatsApp. The delivery date is in the contract and we stick to it. The schedule also lists the approval dates on your side, because late approvals move the delivery date, and that keeps the timeline realistic for both parties.

Do you take over an app another company started but did not finish?

Yes. We start with a technical review of the code, server and accounts, then tell you honestly whether finishing it is cheaper or whether parts need rebuilding. We need the accounts and code in your name before starting, and if they are not, we help you recover or transfer them. You receive the technical review in writing, so it is useful even if you decide to finish with another provider.

Do you handle publishing to the App Store and Google Play?

Yes. We prepare the store listings, screenshots, descriptions and privacy policy, and handle review feedback until the app is approved. We open the accounts in your company's name and flag early the documents Apple and Google require for business accounts. Preparing those documents early prevents launch delays of several weeks caused by paperwork that has nothing to do with development.
