Who is this for?

  • Car rental companies and offices renting by the day, week and month
  • Long-term leasing companies supplying fleets to businesses and organisations
  • Multi-branch rental companies moving cars between cities and airports
  • Offices renting luxury cars, equipment and light trucks
  • Companies running contracts in one program, fines in a spreadsheet and maintenance in a notebook

What you get

Fleet register and status per car

Each car’s details, plate, class, odometer, branch and status: available, rented, reserved, in maintenance or suspended.

Booking and availability board

A calendar showing every car along a timeline, preventing double booking and suggesting an alternative in the same class, with booking from the website or app.

The rental contract

Renter and additional driver details, period, rate, mileage allowance, insurance and deposit, with extensions and early returns calculated automatically.

Photo check-out and return

An inspection from a phone at departure and return: odometer, fuel, photos of every side and the customer’s signature, so any damage claim is settled with evidence.

Pricing and packages

Daily, weekly and monthly rates per class, seasons and offers, and charges for extra mileage, delivery, an additional driver and one-way returns.

Maintenance and documents

Servicing due by mileage or date, a fault log with costs, and alerts before each car’s registration, insurance and periodic inspection expire.

Fines and damage

Each fine is recorded with its date and time, so the system identifies the contract active at that moment and adds it, with damage and accidents, to the customer’s claim.

Collections and invoices

Advance payments, deposits and settlement at close, payment links, tax invoices, and receivables from individuals and companies by age.

Operating and profitability reports

Utilisation per car and class, revenue against maintenance and depreciation, idle cars, and contracts overdue for return.

Where a rental company’s profit leaks

A parked car earns nothing, and a rented car can still lose money. That is the rental equation. The first leak is available cars that were never offered because the branch clerk couldn’t see what had come back to the other branch. The second is traffic fines that arrive two months later, when nobody knows who was driving at that hour, so the company absorbs them. The third is a scratch that wasn’t documented at departure, so there is no one to charge at return.

The fourth is a service that slipped until it became a breakdown, and insurance that lapsed without anyone noticing. The system closes these in order: shared availability across branches, automatic matching of a fine’s date to the active contract, a signed photo inspection at every check-out and return, and maintenance and document alerts that arrive before the date, not after.

Ready-made rental software or a custom system?

There are ready-made programs for rental offices that cover the contract, booking and fleet on a monthly subscription. For a single-branch office with a few dozen cars and ordinary daily rental, they are a sensible and cheaper start. Ask the vendor about exporting your data and about any fee per car or user before you commit.

A custom system shows its value with scale and variety: several branches moving cars between them, long-term corporate leasing with monthly contracts, invoices and replacement cars, pricing with rules of your own, or a website and app under your name where customers book, upload documents and extend their contract. You own the code and the data, and the price doesn’t rise with every car you add to the fleet.

What the first version contains

We start with what runs the branch’s actual day: who walks in to book, who collects a car, and who brings one back. Once that is stable we add long-term leasing, website and app booking, and advanced reports.

  • The register of cars, classes and rates
  • The availability board and booking from the branch
  • The rental contract with printing, extension and closing
  • Photo check-out and return inspection from a phone
  • Fines and damage posted to the contract, collections, receipts and the tax invoice

Regulatory requirements and integrations

Car rental in Egypt is a licensed activity overseen by the Transport General Authority, with requirements covering contracts, how they are documented and the data they must carry. What applies to your business is for you to confirm with the Authority; we build the system around what you decide: contract fields, required documents and what must be retained. Automatic integration with any official platform is built where the platform offers an interface to your business, and we verify that before signing.

A rental company issues invoices, so we build invoicing to meet ZATCA’s e-invoicing requirements. Payment by mada and Apple Pay goes through a licensed payment gateway. Renters’ IDs and driving licences are personal data, handled with the Personal Data Protection Law in mind: role-based permissions, encryption and a log of who viewed what.

  • Vehicle trackers: location and odometer readings if the tracker provider offers an API
  • Traffic fines: manual entry or file import, with direct integration where available
  • Revenue and expenses per car posted to accounting
  • WhatsApp reminders for the return date and amounts due

Migrating open contracts and going live

On go-live day you have cars on the road under active contracts. So before switching we migrate the fleet register with odometers and documents, open contracts with their balances and deposits, customer records and uncollected fines. Old closed contracts stay in the archive, or move as a summary for customer history.

We go live in one branch first. Every new contract there opens on the system, and old contracts are closed as their cars come back. Within weeks the whole fleet is on the system without a day of downtime. Check-out staff are trained on the photo inspection in particular, because it is the step people tend to skip when it gets busy.

Common mistakes in rental systems

The first mistake is treating fines as a detail to add later. They are one of the biggest sources of disputes with customers and must be designed in from the start: date and time, matching to the contract, notice to the customer, and collection. The second is separating maintenance from rental, so a car is booked out when its service is due tomorrow.

The third is settling for a revenue report. A rental company is run on utilisation and on profit per car after maintenance and depreciation, which is what tells you when to sell a car and when to buy another. When comparing quotes, ask: is there a fee per car? Are the website and app included? Are the code and the database in my name?

Frequently asked questions

How much does car rental software cost?

A car rental system with us starts from EGP 282,000; the final price depends on the booking platform and the management system, and on an iOS and Android booking app if you need one. We send a written quote within 24 hours once we know your fleet size, branches and type of rental.

Does the system identify who is liable for a traffic fine?

Yes. When a fine is recorded with its date, time and plate, the system matches it to the contract active at that moment and adds it to the renter’s account with a notice. If it happened while the car was between contracts or with an employee, that shows too. Fines are entered manually or from a file, and direct integration is built where an interface is available.

Is the system compliant with Transport General Authority requirements?

We build the system around the requirements you confirm with the Transport General Authority, from contract data and documents to what must be recorded. We don’t give regulatory advice and we don’t claim accreditation from any body. Automatic integration with any official platform is built where the platform offers an interface to your business, and we verify it before writing it into the contract.

Does it document the car’s condition at check-out and return?

Yes. The employee opens the inspection form on a phone: odometer, fuel level, photos of every side, notes, and the customer’s signature on screen. At return, the new inspection is compared with the old one and any damage is recorded with its photo and cost against the contract. This record is what settles a dispute with the customer or the insurer.

Does it support long-term corporate leasing?

Yes. One contract for a company can cover several cars with monthly terms and rates, recurring invoices, a mileage allowance, maintenance included or excluded, and a replacement car in case of breakdown. The client company can see its cars, invoices and fines in its own portal if you wish.

Can customers book and pay from the website or app?

Yes. The customer chooses the class, dates and pick-up and return branches, sees the final price, uploads documents and pays by mada or Apple Pay through a licensed payment gateway. They only see what is truly available, because the website and app read from the same availability board the branch works on.

Does it connect to vehicle trackers?

If your tracker provider offers an API, we read each car’s location and odometer from it, so mileage is updated and servicing is scheduled without manual entry. The devices and their subscriptions are bought from the provider and are outside the software quote.

Does it support several branches and moving cars between them?

Yes. Each branch has its fleet, staff and cash box, and a car can be collected at one branch and returned at another for a fee you set. Moving a car between branches is recorded as its own movement, so management knows where every car is now and each branch’s utilisation.

How long does implementation take?

8 to 14 weeks. We start with the fleet, bookings, contracts and check-out and return, then maintenance, fines and collections, then website booking, reports and long-term leasing if you need it. The delivery date is written into the contract and we keep it.

Do the code and the data belong to our company?

Yes. The source code, the database, and the hosting and app store accounts are in your company’s name, and the contract says so. There are no fees per car, user or branch. After launch you get fast technical support, and you can later develop the system with any team you choose.
