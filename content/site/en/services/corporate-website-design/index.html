What should an established company’s website achieve?

An established company does not need a website to show that it exists. It needs one that proves its scale. Most visitors have already heard of you: a procurement manager comparing three suppliers, a foreign partner checking before a meeting, an engineer deciding whether to apply. Each is looking for different proof, and a good site gets them there in two clicks.

So we start by naming the visitors that matter and the action you want from each, then build the navigation and pages around those journeys. The result is a site that measures success by qualified enquiries reaching the right department, not by page count or a striking hero image.

  • The client: what the service is, what similar work you have done, and how to request a quote
  • The procurement committee: company details, the licences and certificates you hold, and the company profile
  • The partner or investor: a credible overview, sectors, leadership and official contacts
  • The applicant: the working environment, open roles and how to apply

The pages and content a corporate website needs

There is no ideal number of pages, but some pages an established company cannot do without. We always prefer one deep page per service over a general page that lists ten services in two lines each, because buyers search Google for a specific service, not for "our services".

We also write the content once in Arabic and once in English, rather than translating word for word. The English reader of a Saudi company’s site is usually a partner, supplier or investor with different questions from the local client, and the page order reflects that.

  • Home: what the company does and for whom, in two lines, then services and proof
  • About: history, leadership, values and official company details
  • Services or sectors: one page per service with its own form
  • Projects: real photos, scope of work and city
  • Careers, news, and a contact page with branches, maps and opening hours

Template or custom design for a company website?

If the company is small, has one line of business and needs five pages in one language, a good template on WordPress or a site builder is enough, costs less and launches faster. We say so plainly in the first meeting and do not sell custom design to anyone who does not need it.

Custom design earns its cost when visitors, departments and languages multiply: a visual identity that does not resemble other sites, a content structure that matches your services rather than the template’s boxes, editing and approval roles, and better speed because the site carries no unused plugins. It is also the better fit if you plan to connect the site to a client portal or an internal system later.

What gets a corporate website found on Google?

Google ranks pages, not websites. A page that answers one specific question, such as a particular service in a particular city, has a better chance than a general one. That is why each service gets its own page, title and description, with structured data that tells Google about the organisation, its services and its branches, and with real attention to mobile load time.

That foundation is what we build and hand over. The ranking itself is something nobody can guarantee, because it depends on how strong your competitors are, how old the content is and how consistently you publish. What we commit to is a site Google can read and index without obstacles, and a report showing what each section needs in order to climb over time.

What should the company prepare before we start?

In established companies the website is rarely delayed by development. It is delayed by approvals: who signs off the copy, and who chooses the project photos? Name one person with the authority to decide from the start and we will save weeks of back-and-forth.

  • Brand guidelines and the logo in an editable format
  • The list of services or sectors, and who owns the content for each
  • Real photos of projects, premises and team, with permission to publish them
  • Official company details and the licences and certificates you want to show
  • The email addresses or departments each kind of request should reach

Common mistakes on corporate websites

The most frequent mistake is writing the site in the company’s language rather than the client’s: general phrases about quality and leadership that answer no question at all. The second is a half-finished English version that stops at the home page, which makes a worse impression than having none.

  • One contact form for every purpose, landing in a general inbox nobody watches
  • Stock photos instead of your own projects and people
  • A company profile PDF uploaded years ago and never replaced
  • A careers page that says "send your CV by email" with no actual vacancies
  • A domain or hosting account registered to a previous agency or a former employee

Frequently asked questions

How much does a corporate website cost?

A company website with us starts from EGP 84,000. The final price depends on pages, languages, copywriting and integrations such as a CRM or booking, and we send it in a written quote within 24 hours.

How long does a corporate website take?

Usually 4 to 8 weeks: one to two weeks for page structure and design, then development and content entry in both languages, then review and launch. The biggest factor is how quickly content is approved inside the company. The delivery date is written into the contract and we keep it, along with what we need from you and when.

Do we need an English version of the site?

If you deal with suppliers, partners or lenders outside the Kingdom, or bid for projects run in English, yes. If all your clients are local, Arabic first is enough, and English can be added later, because we build the site from the start to accept a second language without a rebuild.

Is a ready-made template enough for us?

It is, if the site is small, in one language and needs no roles or connections to other systems. A template is quicker and cheaper, but it imposes its shape on your content and its plugins need looking after. We review what you need and give a clear opinion, even when the template is the better fit for your stage.

Do you write the website content?

Yes, on request. You give us the core facts about each service and project, we write them in clear Arabic and natural English, and you approve the copy before it goes live. The quote states how many pages of writing are included. What we do not do is invent figures or achievements: everything published comes from your own information.

Can our team update the site after handover?

Yes. You get an Arabic dashboard where the team adds news, projects and vacancies and edits text and images, with roles that separate who writes from who approves. A short video covers each task. Changes that touch the design or add new features are estimated in a clear quote before any work starts.

Does the company own the domain and the code?

Yes. The domain, hosting and Google accounts are registered to the company, and the full source code is handed over. If the current domain sits with a previous provider, we help you move it before launch. This is written into the contract, and after handover you can continue with any team you choose.

What about visitors’ and applicants’ data?

Forms collect personal data, so we build them with the Personal Data Protection Law in mind: clear consent, a privacy policy, an encrypted connection, and defined access to requests and retention of CVs. We recommend having your legal adviser review the policy wording. After launch you get fast technical support for anything that goes wrong.
