What should a factory’s website achieve?

A factory’s buyer is not a shopper. It is an engineer matching a specification, a procurement officer collecting three quotes, or a trader looking for a new line to distribute. All of them want the same thing: accurate specifications they can copy into a document, and a quick way to ask for a price. If the site does not provide them, they ask the supplier that does.

So a factory site is judged by two questions. Can an engineer find the product that matches their specification in under a minute? And does a quote request reach sales with everything needed to price it, without a clarification call? Every design decision serves those two questions.

The pages and content a factory website needs

The product page is the heart of the site. We build it on one template per category so that specifications are presented consistently and your team can add a new item in minutes. Around the catalogue sit the pages that answer questions of trust: who you are, and what capacity and certification you have.

We show only the certificates and approvals the factory actually holds, as you supply them, with their validity dates. Prices can be shown if you want, though most factories prefer quote requests because the price changes with quantity and customer.

  • Products: categories, and a page per product with specifications and datasheet
  • Sectors or applications: which products suit which use
  • The factory: capacity, production lines and quality control, with real photos
  • Certificates and approvals, and a download library
  • Distributors: where to buy, and a distribution request form
  • Quote request, and contacts for local sales and export

Template, online store or custom catalogue?

A factory with ten products is well served by a brochure site on a good template, with products shown as ordinary pages and the catalogue file available to download. Nothing more is needed, and we say so to anyone who asks.

Some consider a ready-made store platform to display products. Those platforms are designed for selling to consumers with a basket and instant payment, and usually do not suit factory sales built on quote requests, quantities and specifications. A custom catalogue earns its cost when products run into the dozens or hundreds and you need filtering by specification, datasheets, a quote list and two languages from a single data source.

What gets a factory’s website found on Google?

Engineers search by product names, sizes and standard numbers, often in English even inside the Kingdom. A product page with the name and specifications as real text, not inside an image or a PDF, is what shows up in those results. That is why we move the catalogue’s content out of the file and onto pages.

We add structured data for products, a title and description for each item in both languages, stable URLs and good image load times. That is what we build. Ranking depends on competition for your product and on time, and we do not promise a position, especially in export markets where competition differs from country to country.

What should you prepare before we start?

Product data is the real project. If it sits in a tidy spreadsheet, everything moves quickly; if it is spread across PDFs and people’s heads, gathering it sets the schedule. We help with a spreadsheet template that we fill in together, then import it in one go.

  • The product spreadsheet: name, category, item code and specifications, in both languages if possible
  • Product photos on a consistent background, and real photos from the factory
  • Datasheets and current certificates as PDFs
  • The list of current distributors and their territories
  • Who receives local quote requests and who receives export enquiries

Common mistakes on factory websites

The most common is a site that is only a cover for a PDF: a home page and a "download catalogue" button. The file does not show up in search the way pages do, is awkward to read on a phone, and tells you nothing about who opened it or what they were looking for.

  • Specifications inside images that cannot be copied or searched
  • A machine-translated English version with wrong technical terms
  • A general contact form instead of a quote request that asks for product and quantity
  • Stock photos of other factories instead of your own production lines
  • Discontinued products still in the catalogue

Frequently asked questions

How much does a factory website cost?

A factory website with us starts from EGP 84,000. The final price depends on the number of products, the languages and the integrations, and we send it in a written quote within 24 hours.

What is the difference between a product catalogue and an online store?

A store sells at a visible price with a basket and instant payment, which suits consumer sales. A catalogue presents the product with its specifications and ends in a quote request, because in business sales the price changes with quantity, customer and supply terms. The two can be combined later, for example by opening direct ordering to selected customers at their own prices.

We have hundreds of products. Do we enter them one by one?

No. We prepare a structured spreadsheet of products and specifications with you and import it in one go. After launch your team adds new items from the dashboard or by updating the spreadsheet. If the data lives in your internal system, it can be synced with the site where the system allows it.

Can datasheets and certificates be downloaded from the site?

Yes. Each product has its files: the technical datasheet, installation instructions and the certificates the factory holds. Some files can sit behind a short form, so sales knows who is interested in which product. Files are replaced from the dashboard when a new version is issued.

Do we need the site in English?

In most cases, yes. Many engineers and consultants write specifications in English even inside the Kingdom, and export markets require it. We build both versions from one data source so that a specification never differs between languages, and your technical team reviews the terminology before publishing.

How do quote requests reach sales?

The buyer collects products and quantities in a list and sends it with their company details. The request arrives by email and WhatsApp or is logged in a CRM, with everything needed to price it. We separate local requests from export enquiries if each has its own team. The forms follow the Personal Data Protection Law.

Is a ready-made template enough for a factory?

It is for a factory with a small number of products that wants a brochure presence and a catalogue file to download. The template runs out with hundreds of items, filtering by specification and two languages. We review the size of your catalogue and say plainly which option fits, and we do not sell custom design to anyone who does not need it.

Who owns the site, and what happens after launch?

The domain, hosting, code and product data are in the factory’s name, and the delivery date is written into the contract and kept. After launch you get fast technical support for any fault and a video guide to managing the catalogue, and you can agree a monthly maintenance plan covering updates and backups.
