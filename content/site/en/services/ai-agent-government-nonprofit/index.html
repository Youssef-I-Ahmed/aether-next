Who is this for?

  • Charities hearing from beneficiaries, donors and volunteers on one number
  • Non-profit foundations and professional associations whose members ask about membership, programmes and events
  • Service bodies answering the same questions on criteria, documents and request status
  • Organisations whose phone is jammed in certain seasons, with a small team
  • Managers who want to know what people actually ask, to improve their services

What you get

Answers from approved information

Services, programmes, opening hours, locations and required documents as the organisation approved them. When it has no answer it says so and refers to a member of staff.

Explains eligibility criteria

It explains the published criteria in plain language and asks a few first questions that help the enquirer see whether to go on with a request and what to prepare.

It decides no eligibility and promises no aid

The decision stays with the committee or the member of staff responsible. The digital employee explains the criteria and takes the request, and tells nobody of an acceptance, a rejection or an amount.

Takes the request and its documents

It collects the request details step by step, accepts photos of documents and checks they are complete, so the request reaches staff ready to be assessed.

Request status

After verifying who is asking, it tells them what stage their request has reached where the organisation’s system offers an interface, and which documents are missing.

Referral to the right department

It routes each enquiry to its department: beneficiaries, donations, volunteering, membership, complaints, with a summary of what the enquirer said.

Donors and volunteers

It answers on open programmes and ways to take part, and points donors to the official donation channels the organisation has approved. It takes no money in the chat.

Voice notes and a phone version

It understands voice notes and dialects, and can answer calls in a natural voice, which helps those who would rather not type.

What the digital employee does in a charity or a service body

Organisations that serve the public hear the same question many times over: what are the criteria, which documents, when does registration open, where has my request got to. The team is usually small, and the person answering the phone is the same person assessing requests, so both fall behind.

The digital employee takes the repeated questions at any hour, takes requests complete, and refers whatever needs a person to the right department. Staff are left free to assess cases and to talk to those who need a direct conversation.

  • Answers questions on services, programmes, criteria, documents and opening hours
  • Takes requests and their documents and checks they are complete
  • Tells the enquirer what stage their request has reached, where the system allows it
  • Points donors and volunteers to the approved channels and programmes
  • Refers complaints and special cases to the member of staff responsible with a summary

Explaining the criteria is one thing; deciding is another

In organisations of this kind, one wrong word can raise the hopes of someone in need or turn away someone entitled. So there is a fixed limit in the setup: the digital employee explains the published criteria as the organisation wrote them and helps the enquirer prepare a request, and tells no one that they are or are not eligible.

Asked about the amount of assistance, a payment date or the outcome of an assessment, it answers only with what is published, and says the decision comes from the organisation once the request has been assessed. It gives no legal opinion and does not interpret regulations; those questions go to the member of staff responsible.

Beneficiaries’ data and privacy

Beneficiaries’ requests carry personal data, some of it sensitive, such as financial, health and family circumstances. We collect only what the request needs and keep it in the project’s own database, with access limited to those entitled to it, in line with the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law.

The digital employee reveals nothing about a request before verifying who is asking, in the way the organisation approves. The number, the accounts, the code and the data belong to the organisation and stay with it.

How it connects to the organisation’s systems

Where the beneficiary management or request system offers an interface, the digital employee records the request in it and reads the request’s stage from it. Where it does not, requests reach staff on a simple panel sorted by department, and status enquiries stay with staff.

Information is updated in one place: when a criterion changes or a new programme opens, the person in charge edits it and the answer changes on WhatsApp, the website and the phone together. The report of repeated questions shows management what needs explaining better on its website and in its announcements.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a digital employee for a charity or service body cost?

A conversational agent starts with us from EGP 37,500 and rises with the number of services and flows and with a request system integration, plus a monthly running cost for hosting, the AI model and follow-up. We send a written quote within 24 hours.

Does it decide who is eligible for assistance?

No. It explains the published criteria, asks a few first questions and takes the request and its documents. The decision stays with the committee or the member of staff responsible, and it tells no enquirer of an acceptance, a rejection or an amount. That limit is fixed in its setup.

Does it understand people who write in plain dialect or send a voice note?

Yes. It understands Saudi and Gulf dialects, Modern Standard Arabic, typos and voice notes, and answers in clear language and short sentences. Before launch we test it on a sample of real messages from your beneficiaries.

Does it take donations?

It takes no money and no card details in the chat. It answers on open programmes and points donors to the official donation channels the organisation has approved, and refers anyone wanting a special donation or a partnership to the person in charge.

Is beneficiaries’ data safe?

It is kept in the project’s own database with defined access, in line with the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law, and we collect only what the request needs. The digital employee reveals nothing about a request before verifying who is asking.

Does it replace our beneficiary services staff?

No. It takes the repeated questions and the collecting of details off them, and leaves them what needs judgement and sympathy: assessing cases and talking to people in special circumstances. Any enquirer who asks for a person is referred to one.

What if the criteria change or a new programme opens?

The person in charge edits the information on a simple panel and the answer changes on every channel at once, with no developer needed. We provide fast technical support after launch for anything that needs a change to the flows themselves.
