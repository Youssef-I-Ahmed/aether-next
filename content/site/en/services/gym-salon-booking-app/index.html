Who is this for?

  • Gyms with monthly and annual memberships, group classes and personal trainers.
  • Yoga, Pilates and CrossFit studios selling class packages with limited places.
  • Salons and beauty centres where the client books with a particular staff member.
  • Spas, massage centres and hammams selling sessions, packages and gift vouchers.
  • Barbershops with several chairs and branches that want to organise waiting.

What you get

Booking by service and staff member

The client picks the service, then the staff member and a time from slots that are really free, and the appointment length follows from the services chosen.

Group classes with capacity

A weekly class timetable, booking a place until the class is full, and a waiting list that promotes the next person automatically on a cancellation.

Memberships and packages

A membership for a period, or a package of sessions drawn down with each visit, with an expiry date and a balance the client can always see.

Renewal and freezing

A reminder before a membership ends, one-tap or automatic renewal, and a freeze request under the policy you set.

QR code entry

A digital membership card scanned at the gate or reception, which records attendance and checks the membership is valid.

Payment and deposits

mada, Apple Pay and cards through a licensed payment gateway, Tabby and Tamara for annual memberships and packages, and a deposit that secures the booking.

Reminders and cancellation policy

A reminder before the appointment with confirm or reschedule, a cancellation window you set, and what follows a no-show without notice.

Loyalty, referrals and gifts

Points on visits, a reward for inviting a friend, and gift vouchers bought and sent from the app.

Admin dashboard and staff schedules

Each staff member’s shifts and leave, commission on their services and sales, and reports on occupancy, expired memberships and lapsed clients.

The problem the app solves

In a salon the phone rings while the stylist has both hands busy with a client, and the booking is lost. A client books and does not show, and the chair sits empty at peak time. In a gym a membership runs out with nobody noticing, so the member does not renew and no one calls, and a class fills up while half of those booked stay at home and others sit on the waiting list.

An app takes bookings around the clock with no staff involved, reminds the client and asks for confirmation, and fills a cancelled place from the waiting list. It turns renewal into something the system manages, not a matter of chance: a reminder before expiry and one-tap renewal. Empty time and unrenewed memberships are the two largest losses in this kind of business.

An app, or booking through the website or Instagram?

A new client finds you on Instagram or Google Maps and books from a link. So a web booking page remains essential, and a new client should not be asked to download an app to book a first visit. A small salon with two chairs may need no more than a booking page and reminder messages.

An app earns its cost when the relationship is a membership or a regular visit: a gym member books classes every week and scans a card at the door, and a salon client books with the same stylist every month and follows the balance of a package. That is where notifications, loyalty and renewals do what a booking link cannot.

  • New and occasional clients: a web booking page.
  • Members, packages and regular visits: an app.
  • One schedule behind both channels, so nothing is double-booked.

What a gym needs and what a salon needs

A gym sells time and capacity: a membership for a period, classes with a number of places, a personal trainer by the session. Its centre of gravity is memberships with their renewal and freezing, the class timetable and waiting lists, and entry at the gate. Its income depends on the renewal rate.

A salon or spa sells the time of a particular person: a service with a duration and a price, with a staff member the client prefers. Its centre of gravity is staff schedules, combining several services in one appointment, commissions and product sales. Its income depends on chair occupancy. We build on one foundation and switch on the modules each business needs. Where there are separate men’s and women’s sections, each has its own schedule, staff and what its clients see.

What the first version should contain

Booking by service and staff member or by class, reminders and confirmation, rescheduling and cancellation within your policy, payment or a deposit, the client account with upcoming and past appointments, and an admin dashboard for schedules. For gyms, add the membership, its validity and the entry code.

Phase two brings loyalty and referrals, gift vouchers, automatic renewal, product sales, and training and nutrition plans. If you plan to sell digital content inside the app, such as recorded workout programmes, the app stores have their own payment policies for that, which we review with you during analysis.

How the app connects to your systems

If you have gym or salon management software, the app reads schedules and memberships from it where the software offers an interface. Where it does not, the app’s dashboard becomes the schedule reception works from, and phone bookings are entered there too, so there is a single source.

Payment goes through a licensed payment gateway, and the tax invoice is issued by the till or an accounting system that meets e-invoicing requirements. Entry gates and code readers are connected where their provider offers an interface; otherwise reception scans the member’s code from a tablet.

  • Gym or salon management software: schedules and memberships.
  • The till, accounting and a licensed payment gateway.
  • Entry gates and code readers.
  • WhatsApp or SMS as a backup reminder channel.

What to prepare, and common mistakes

Prepare the list of services with their durations and prices, the staff, what each one offers and their working hours, the types of membership and package and their terms, and a written cancellation, freeze and refund policy. A vague policy is the source of most disputes with clients after launch.

The most common mistakes: a schedule staff do not keep up to date, so a client books a time that is not free. Then accepting bookings at peak times with no deposit and no cancellation window. Then neglecting the member after sign-up, when the app shows who has stopped attending so you can reach them before the renewal date, not after it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a booking app for a salon or gym cost?

A booking app with us starts from EGP 180,000; the final price depends on the number of branches and services and the membership types. We send a written quote within 24 hours once we know your branches and services.

How long does the app take to build?

From 8 to 14 weeks for the first release. A booking app for one branch sits near the lower end; memberships, packages, several branches and a link to entry gates move it towards the upper end. The delivery date is written into the contract and we keep it, and we try the system in one branch with staff before inviting clients.

Does the app support memberships, packages and automatic renewal?

Yes. A membership for a period or a package of sessions, with the balance and expiry date visible to the client. They get a reminder before it ends and renew in one tap, and automatic renewal from a saved card can be switched on through the payment gateway, with advance notice. A freeze request is made in the app under your policy.

How does the app reduce no-shows?

With three tools: a reminder with a confirm or reschedule button, a deposit or cancellation window you set, and a waiting list that fills the cancelled place. We do not promise a percentage, since it varies with the business and the policy, but the reports show you the no-show rate before and after each tool is switched on.

Can the client choose the staff member they want?

Yes, and in salons that is essential. They choose the service, then the staff member or the first one available, and see real free times. You can hide staff selection for certain services. Each staff member’s commission is worked out from their services automatically, and management sees each one’s occupancy and revenue.

Can it connect to the gym’s entry gates?

Yes, where the gate provider offers an interface: the member scans a code, the gate opens if the membership is valid, and attendance is recorded. We review the existing devices before contracting. Where integration is not available, reception scans the code from a tablet, which checks validity and records attendance.

Which payment methods are available?

mada, cards and Apple Pay through a licensed payment gateway, with Tabby and Tamara for spreading annual memberships and packages, and payment at the branch. Funds settle directly to your business account. Activating the gateway needs a commercial registration and a bank account, and we start the paperwork when the project starts.

I have a men’s section and a women’s section. Is that supported?

Yes. Each section has its own services, staff, schedule, classes and opening hours, and a client sees only what belongs to their section. Management sees reports for each section separately and combined, with permissions that set which staff can view what.

Do I own the app and the code, or is it a monthly subscription?

You own it. The code, store accounts, server and client database are in your business’s name under the contract, unlike booking platforms that charge a subscription per branch or staff member and list you next to competitors. After launch you pay the running costs and a maintenance contract if you choose one, and we provide fast technical support.
