Start with the problem, not the technology

Most AI projects that stall start with the question “how do we use AI?”. The ones that succeed start with a different question: where is our team losing time, or where are we losing customers? When the problem is clear and measurable, choosing the solution is easy, and judging its success after launch becomes possible.

Here is a short map linking the most common business problems to the right solution for each. Many companies find themselves in two or three lines, and that is where we start, with the highest impact and lowest complexity.

  • Lots of WhatsApp messages and slow replies: a WhatsApp AI agent
  • Visitors land on the website and leave without getting in touch: a website chatbot
  • Missed calls and a busy phone line: a voice agent that answers calls
  • Data moved by hand between systems, email and spreadsheets: workflow automation
  • Invoices, contracts and documents read and keyed in by hand: document AI
  • Staff asking HR, finance and IT the same questions: an internal assistant
  • Quality, safety or stock that needs eyes on it: computer vision
  • Decisions made on late or conflicting reports: dashboards and business intelligence

Customer-facing solutions and team-facing solutions

The solutions fall into two groups. The first faces customers directly: the WhatsApp agent, the website chatbot and the voice agent. Their return shows quickly in reply speed, qualified leads and evening and weekend coverage, but they need more care over tone and the limits of what the bot says, because customers see every mistake.

The second group serves the team internally: automation, document reading, the internal assistant, computer vision and dashboards. Their return shows in hours saved, fewer errors and faster decisions, and the risk is lower because the user is an employee who can be trained and can check the result. That is why many companies start with an internal solution to build trust, then move on to customer-facing ones.

The two groups often work together: the bot collects the customer’s request, automation logs it in the CRM and alerts the salesperson, and the dashboard shows management how many requests came in and how many closed. That connection is what turns AI from an isolated experiment into part of how the company works.

What makes an AI project succeed?

The AI model itself is now available to everyone, and the difference between a successful project and a failed one is rarely the model. It lies in the information it is fed, the rules that govern it, the systems it is connected to, and how its impact is measured.

  • Approved information: answers, prices and policies reviewed by someone at your company, not generic web content
  • Clear limits: what the solution handles alone, and what it always hands to a person
  • System integration: a solution that cannot reach your CRM, booking or accounting system is only half a solution
  • A success measure before you start: response time, share resolved automatically, hours saved or extraction accuracy
  • Regular review: reading samples of conversations and results in the first weeks and improving the replies

How to compare AI companies

AI quotes vary enormously because the word is used for very different things, from a ready-made bot template to a system built on your processes. So ask every company to answer the same questions in writing, and compare the answers, not just the numbers.

  • What specific problem will the project solve, and how will we measure success?
  • Which systems will the solution connect to, and is the integration included in the price?
  • What is the monthly cost after launch: hosting, model usage, channel fees such as Meta?
  • Who owns the accounts, data and code, and can the solution move to another provider?
  • What does the solution do when it does not know the answer? Can we test it on our data before launch?
  • What support comes after launch, and who updates the information when our prices or policies change?

Frequently asked questions

How much do AI solutions for business cost?

It depends on the solution: automating one process between two systems costs far less than a voice agent connected to a booking system or a computer vision project across several cameras. A chatbot with us starts from EGP 37,500, with the final price set by the solution and the systems connected, plus a monthly running cost for hosting and model usage. We send a written quote within 24 hours once we understand the problem and the systems to connect.

Where do I start if I am not sure which solution I need?

Start with the work that repeats most and takes longest: answering enquiries, data entry or compiling reports. In a short online meeting we review these with you, rank them by impact and ease of delivery, and propose a single first phase whose result can be measured within weeks, instead of a large project with an unknown return.

Does AI understand Saudi Arabic?

Yes. Modern models understand Saudi and Gulf dialects and MSA, and handle messages mixed with English, typos and voice notes. We test every solution on real samples of your customers’ messages or your documents before launch, and add your service names, branches and industry terms so the answers are accurate and on-brand.

Does AI make up wrong answers?

It can if left without guardrails, which is why we build solutions to answer only from your approved information and to apologise and hand off to staff when they cannot find the answer. In internal solutions such as document reading, staff review uncertain results before they are approved. We read samples of conversations and results in the first weeks to improve accuracy.

Is my company’s data safe?

We store data in a database dedicated to your project with defined permissions, comply with the Saudi Personal Data Protection Law, and can host in the region on request. The quote explains what is sent to the AI model and what stays inside your systems, and we sign a non-disclosure agreement if you wish. The accounts, data and code remain your company’s property.

Can I try a solution before signing?

Yes. On our /en/try page there are live chat and voice agents you can question in your own words. Once we agree, we build a first version on your real data that you and your team test before it reaches customers, and we only move to launch after you approve the result.

Will AI replace my staff?

In most companies it does not replace them; it takes repetitive work off their plate. The bot answers recurring questions and hands the salesperson a ready lead, and document reading saves hours of manual entry so the accountant can focus on review. The usual result is that the same team serves more customers and transactions at higher quality, without extra pressure.

What is the difference between automation and AI?

Automation runs fixed steps: when a request arrives, log it in the system and send an alert. AI adds understanding: reading a message written in the customer’s own words, extracting data from a photographed invoice, or summarising a conversation. The best results come from combining the two: AI understands the input, and automation makes sure the result reaches the right place.

Do you connect the solution to our existing systems?

Yes, and it is a core part of every project. We connect solutions to CRM, booking, accounting and ERP systems, email, WhatsApp and spreadsheets through their APIs or automation tools. If you do not have a suitable system, we can build a simple one connected to the solution, such as a sales CRM or an order tracking dashboard.

How long does a first AI project take?

Automating a single process can be live within one to two weeks, a basic chatbot within 3 to 6 weeks, while computer vision projects need 6 to 12 weeks. The timeline is written into the contract with on-time delivery guaranteed, you follow progress weekly in the client portal, and we provide fast technical support after launch.
