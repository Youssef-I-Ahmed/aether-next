Who is this for?

  • Online stores with repeat buyers: perfume, beauty, fashion, coffee and baby supplies.
  • Stores on Salla, Zid or Shopify that have grown and want an app in their own name without leaving the platform.
  • Brands with branches and a store that want one loyalty programme across in-store and online purchases.
  • Stores spending heavily on ads to win back customers who have already bought from them.
  • Distributors and wholesalers that want an ordering app for their retail customers.

The problem a store app solves

An online store pays to bring a customer in the first time, then pays again to bring them back: a retargeting ad, an SMS, a WhatsApp campaign. The higher ad costs climb, the thinner the margin. A customer who has bought from you three times should cost you nothing the fourth time.

An app gives you a free channel to that customer: a notification on their phone, an icon with your name in front of them, and a purchase in seconds because the address and card are saved. That is why an app serves stores with real repeat purchasing, and does little for a store selling something people buy once in a lifetime.

Is your store ready for an app?

The responsive store website remains the acquisition channel: it ranks on Google, ads land on it, and it asks for no download. The app does not replace it; it is added for your loyal customers. The practical question is what share of your orders comes from people who have bought before.

If that share is small and the store is in its first months, the money is better spent on the product, advertising and the website. If you have a base of customers who come back, the app raises how often they buy and lowers what it costs to win them back. We look at your store’s numbers with you before quoting, and advise waiting if they do not justify an app.

  • The share of orders from returning customers.
  • Mobile visits and the mobile checkout completion rate.
  • What you spend each month winning back past customers.

Web View or a custom-built app?

Web View apps show your store’s website inside an app frame. They are the cheapest and fastest option and give you an icon and notifications, but the experience is still a website: slower, dependent on the connection, and stores may be strict about accepting them if they offer nothing beyond the site. They work as a first test of how willing your customers are to download.

A custom-built app builds the interface for the phone itself and reads data from your store through its API. It is faster and smoother, works partly offline, and allows features such as camera search, barcode scanning and one-tap checkout. It costs more, and what justifies it is a sales volume that moves when the purchase rate improves.

How the app connects to a store on Salla, Zid or Shopify

If your store is custom-built, we build the app on its API or add one. If it runs on a platform such as Salla, Zid or Shopify, integration is possible within what the platform exposes through its interfaces: products, categories, stock, customers and orders. Whatever the platform does not expose, such as some checkout steps, is done the way the platform supports.

So we begin by reviewing your plan and your platform’s interfaces before any quote, and write into the scope what will work through direct integration and what has an alternative. Your store dashboard remains the source of truth: you add a product once and it appears on the site and in the app, and an order from any channel enters the same list and passes through the same stock, shipping and invoice.

  • Products, prices and stock: from the store to the app.
  • Orders and customers: from the app to the store.
  • Shipping and tax invoices: stay in the store system.
  • Notifications and loyalty: managed from the app’s dashboard.

What the first version contains, and what to prepare

The first version: browsing and search, the product page, basket and checkout, the customer account and orders, shipment tracking, notifications, and banners managed from the dashboard. Loyalty, wallet and interactive content come after you see who downloads the app and how they buy.

  • Access to the store or platform interfaces, and your current plan.
  • Your brand identity and product images at a quality suited to mobile.
  • Developer accounts in your business’s name on the App Store and Google Play.
  • A plan to invite customers to download, such as an app-only coupon.

Common mistakes with store apps

The first is treating the app as an acquisition channel. Few people download the app of a store they have never bought from. The app is for keeping customers, and its success is measured by repeat purchases, not by downloads. The second is sending so many notifications that customers switch them off or delete the app.

The third is stock that is out of sync: a customer pays for an item that has sold out. We test the sync under the load of seasonal offers before launch. The fourth is ignoring store review: an app that only shows a website with no added value may be rejected, so we design from the start for what passes review.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an ecommerce app cost?

An ecommerce app with us starts from EGP 180,000; the final price depends on your store platform and the features you need. We send a written quote within 24 hours after reviewing your store and its platform.

Can you build an app for my store on Salla or Zid?

Yes, within the interfaces the platform offers on your plan. We read products and stock and write orders to your store, so you keep managing from the platform’s dashboard. We check what is actually available before contracting and write it into the scope, and tell you what will be done by an alternative route. We do not promise an integration we have not verified.

Do I need to re-enter my products in the app?

No. The app reads from your store directly: you add a product, change its price or pause it from your current dashboard and the app follows. Orders from the app enter the store’s own order list, so the way your team picks, packs and ships does not change.

What is the difference between a Web View app and a custom-built app?

A Web View app shows your store’s website inside an app: cheaper and quicker, but relatively slow, limited in features, and it may be rejected in store review if it offers nothing new. A custom-built app has interfaces made for the phone and reads data from your store: faster, smoother and richer in features, at a higher cost. We suggest whichever fits your sales volume.

Will an app increase my sales?

We do not promise a number. An app is a retention tool: it makes buying again easier, gives you free notifications and shortens checkout. Its effect shows in stores that already have returning customers and fades where there is no repeat purchasing. We agree clear measures with you to track after launch: downloads, repeat purchases and order value.

Which payment methods does the app offer?

mada, cards and Apple Pay, Tabby and Tamara for instalments, and cash on delivery if you offer it, through a licensed payment gateway. For custom-built stores we connect your gateway directly. For stores on platforms, payment is taken the way the platform supports, so orders and settlements stay where they are.

How long does the app take?

From 6 to 12 weeks. A store with a ready, well-organised API sits near the lower end; adding loyalty, a wallet and a fully custom design moves it towards the upper end. The timeline includes App Store and Google Play review. The delivery date is written into the contract and we keep it.

Is the app in my store’s name, and do I own the code?

Yes. It is published from developer accounts in your business’s name, and the source code is yours under the contract, so your app is not tied to a monthly subscription with us. That is an important difference from ready-made subscription apps, where the app stops if you stop paying.

What happens if I move my store to another platform later?

We build the app with an integration layer that is separate from its screens. If you move from one platform to another, or to a custom-built store, we rewrite the integration layer without rebuilding the app, and the same app stays on your customers’ phones with their accounts. That costs far less than a new app.
