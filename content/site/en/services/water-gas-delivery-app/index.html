Water and Gas Delivery App Development for Recurring Orders

Get a quote on WhatsAppRequest a quote

A water or gas delivery app is built around the recurring order: a customer who asks for the same item every week or two, and a driver who serves the same district on a fixed route. It consists of a customer app for one-tap ordering or a scheduled subscription, a driver app with the daily route, proof of delivery and cash collection, and an admin dashboard for zones, prices and stock. We build it for water bottling plants and distributors, gas distributors and any business that delivers periodic orders. A first release takes 10 to 16 weeks. A water or gas delivery app with us starts from EGP 180,000; the final price depends on the number of trucks and zones, live tracking and integrations. You get a written quote within 24 hours.

Who is this for?

  • Water bottling plants and stations distributing bottles and cartons to homes and offices.
  • Drinking-water distributors working with refillable gallons, returnable containers and coupons.
  • Gas cylinder distributors that take orders by phone and share them out between trucks.
  • Water tanker companies and periodic tank-filling services.
  • Similar periodic delivery businesses: dairy, ice, laundry and office supplies.

What you get

One-tap order and repeat last order

There are few items and the customer orders the same thing, so the first screen offers buttons to repeat the last order to the saved address.

Subscriptions and scheduled delivery

Delivery every week or two on a fixed day and time, which the customer can change or pause while travelling, without a call.

Prepaid packages and coupons

The customer buys a package of, say, twenty bottles at a lower price, the balance is drawn down with each delivery and the remainder is always visible.

Returnable containers and cylinders

What was collected empty and delivered full is recorded for each customer, with the deposit value, so you know where your containers are and how many are out.

Daily driver routes

Orders and subscriptions are shared between trucks by zone and load, and stops are ordered to cut distance and fuel.

Driver app

The day’s deliveries in order, navigation to the address, proof of delivery by photo or code, and cash or card-terminal collection with an end-of-day statement.

Zones, time windows and minimums

Service zones drawn on the map, delivery days per district, time windows, a minimum order and delivery fees.

Online payment and pay on delivery

mada, Apple Pay and cards through a licensed payment gateway, payment to the driver, and credit accounts for offices and companies with a monthly invoice.

Admin dashboard and reports

The day’s orders on the map, each truck’s load and stock, driver collections, and customers who have stopped ordering.

The problem the app solves

Most water and gas distributors run on the phone, WhatsApp and the driver’s notebook. An order is noted in a message, passed to the driver by word of mouth, and collections are checked from memory at the end of the day. Nobody notices the customer who used to order every week and stopped, and bottles and cylinders leak away with no record of who still has them.

Because demand is recurring and predictable, this is one of the businesses that gains most from an app. A scheduled subscription turns a customer into steady income without them having to remember to order, a daily route serves more customers on the same fuel, and a container ledger stops a silent loss. And the customer orders in seconds instead of making a call that may go unanswered.

How it differs from a food delivery app

A food delivery app is built on an instant order assigned to the nearest driver and delivered within minutes. Water and gas delivery is the opposite: orders are collected, then spread over routes, the driver carries stock on the truck, and the customer is fixed and recurring. Using a food delivery template here gives you an app that works against the way you operate.

So we build different logic: delivery days per district, the day’s orders grouped and ordered geographically, the truck’s load and stock, returns, subscriptions, and credit collection for offices. Live tracking matters less here than a simple notice: the driver will reach you today between this time and that.

  • Orders grouped into routes, not instant assignment per order.
  • Stock on the truck, counted at loading and on return.
  • Returnable bottles and cylinders with a balance per customer.
  • Subscriptions and prepaid packages.

An app, or ordering through WhatsApp?

Ordering through WhatsApp works at the start, and there is nothing wrong with beginning there. It can be organised with an automated agent that takes the order and logs it in a dashboard that assigns drivers, a lower-cost first step that proves the volume of demand.

The app pulls ahead as the customer base grows: one-tap reorder is quicker than typing a message, a scheduled subscription needs no message at all, and the customer sees the package balance and containers held. More importantly, the driver app and the admin dashboard are where most of the operational return comes from, and you can start with those even before the customer app.

What the first version should contain

We usually suggest starting from the inside: an admin dashboard that logs orders from every channel and assigns them, and a driver app with the route, proof of delivery and collection. Then the customer app with ordering, reorder, addresses and payment. In that order, operations improve from the first week, even before customers download the app.

Phase two brings scheduled subscriptions, prepaid packages, automatic route ordering and credit accounts for companies. If your business is gas, its distribution is a regulated activity with licensing and safety conditions that your business confirms with the regulator, and we build what is needed to document them.

How the app connects to your systems

What the driver loads in the morning leaves the warehouse stock, and what comes back returns to it. So the app is connected to the inventory and accounting system where it offers an interface: truck loading, sales, returns, and each driver’s collections and handover.

Tax invoices are issued by an accounting system that meets e-invoicing requirements, or we build invoice issuing into the project if you have no system. If your trucks carry tracking devices, their positions can be read where the provider offers an interface, instead of relying on the driver’s phone alone.

  • Inventory: truck loading and returns.
  • Accounting and e-invoicing.
  • A licensed payment gateway and mobile card terminals.
  • Map and messaging services, billed by usage.

What to prepare, and common mistakes

Prepare the list of items and prices, service zones and the days for each district, the number of trucks and their capacity, how you collect payment today, and your container and deposit policy. If you have a customer list in notebooks or Excel, it is the most valuable thing you own, and we import it with its addresses.

The most common mistakes: a polished customer app with no driver app, so operations stay as they were. Then inaccurate addresses that waste the driver’s time, which we fix by pinning the location on the map at the first delivery. Then promising a delivery time more precise than the route can bear. A clear time window the driver keeps is the honest option.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a water or gas delivery app cost?

A water or gas delivery app with us starts from EGP 180,000; the final price depends on live tracking and integrations. We send a written quote within 24 hours once we know the number of trucks and zones.

How long does the app take to build?

From 10 to 16 weeks for a first release with the customer app, driver app and admin dashboard. The dashboard and driver app can go live first in fewer weeks, followed by the customer app. The delivery date is written into the contract and we keep it, and we try the system on one route before rolling it out.

Does the app support subscriptions and scheduled delivery?

Yes, and it is one of its most important features. The customer picks the quantity, day and frequency, the order joins that day’s route automatically without being placed, and a notice goes out before delivery. They can postpone a delivery or pause the subscription. Payment comes from a prepaid package balance, a saved card or cash to the driver.

How does it handle empty bottles and gas cylinders?

At each delivery the driver records what was handed over full and what was collected empty, building a container balance and a deposit amount for every customer. Management knows how many containers are out and with whom, and differences show up when the truck is counted at the end of the day. That ledger stops a leak that is hard to see in notebooks.

Does the app plan driver routes automatically?

Yes. The day’s orders are shared between trucks by zone and load, and stops are ordered to cut distance, with the supervisor able to adjust by hand. In the first version, assignment by district and a simple order is usually enough, and we add more precise automatic routing as daily deliveries grow.

Does the driver need a separate app?

Yes, and it is the part that really improves operations. It holds the delivery list in order, navigation, proof of delivery, returns, collection, and an end-of-day statement of what was collected and what is left on the truck. We design it with large buttons and few steps, and it works on a weak connection and syncs afterwards.

What are the payment and collection options?

Online payment by mada, cards and Apple Pay through a licensed payment gateway, cash or a mobile card terminal with the driver, a prepaid package balance, and a credit account for companies with a monthly invoice. Every cash collection is recorded against the driver and matched at handover, so you know what each one is holding.

Does it suit businesses other than water and gas?

Yes. The same logic serves any periodic delivery with few items and fixed customers: dairy, ice, laundry, office supplies and tanker filling. We adjust the items, the pricing method and returns to your business, and the basis stays the same: subscriptions, routes and collection.

Do I own the code and the app?

Yes. The source code, store accounts, server and customer database are in your business’s name under the contract, and we take no commission on your orders. After launch you pay the running costs directly to the service providers, and a maintenance contract if you choose one. We provide fast technical support, which matters for a business whose drivers work every day.
