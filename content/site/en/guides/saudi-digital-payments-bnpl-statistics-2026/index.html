What share of payments in Saudi Arabia are electronic?

The Saudi Central Bank measures the share of electronic payments in the number of retail payment transactions made by individuals. It is one of the Financial Sector Development Program’s objectives, and it rose 15 percentage points in two years.

For a business owner, cash is now the exception. A shop, restaurant or service centre that does not accept electronic payment easily loses customers who do not carry cash at all.

  • Electronic payments were 85% of total retail payment transactions by individuals in 2025 (Saudi Central Bank, 2025).
  • The share was 79% in 2024 and 70% in 2023 (Saudi Central Bank).
  • Electronic transactions rose to about 14.6 billion in 2025, from 12.6 billion in 2024 and 10.8 billion in 2023 (Saudi Central Bank).
  • The central bank attributes the growth mainly to the mada system, especially at points of sale and in e-commerce (Saudi Central Bank, 2025).

Points of sale and cash: where Saudi consumers spend

Consumer spending is growing, and most of it flows through point-of-sale terminals and e-commerce while cash withdrawals decline. Cash is still a large number, though, because some customers and businesses still use it.

That is why a restaurant or retail point-of-sale system needs to accept mada, cards and digital wallets smoothly while still recording cash accurately.

  • Consumer spending in Saudi Arabia reached EGP 23,548.5 billion in 2025, up 11% from about EGP 21,276 billion in 2024 (Saudi Central Bank, as reported by Okaz).
  • Point-of-sale sales reached about EGP 10,608 billion in 2025, up 6% (Saudi Central Bank, 2025).
  • Point-of-sale sales in 2025 came from 11.5 billion transactions on 2.3 million terminals (Saudi Central Bank, 2025).
  • ATM cash withdrawals fell about 3% to roughly EGP 8,062.5 billion in 2025 (Saudi Central Bank, 2025).

Electronic payment in online commerce

The fastest-growing part of electronic payment is paying online. mada e-commerce sales grew more than ten times as fast as point-of-sale sales.

The figure covers mada cards only and excludes credit cards, so total online electronic payment is larger. In practice, enabling mada in your store is not an add-on; it is the minimum.

  • E-commerce sales on mada cards rose to EGP 4,878 billion in 2025, up 65% on 2024 (Saudi Central Bank).
  • These sales came from 1.77 billion transactions in 2025 (Saudi Central Bank).

Buy now, pay later (BNPL) in Saudi Arabia by the numbers

Buy now, pay later is a regulated activity in Saudi Arabia that needs a Saudi Central Bank licence, and new licences have followed one another through 2025 and 2026. The central bank does not yet publish an updated annual figure for BNPL volume, so we relied on announced licences and the companies’ own figures.

Read company figures with care: a "registered user" is not necessarily an active one, and the numbers cover more than one country. Still, they show that paying in instalments has become familiar to a large share of Gulf shoppers.

  • The Saudi Central Bank licensed Tabby Finance for BNPL activity in late 2025, bringing the number of licensed finance companies at the time to 68 (Saudi Central Bank, 2025).
  • Licensed finance companies reached 76 in July 2026, after a new company was licensed for BNPL (Saudi Central Bank, as reported by Arab News in July 2026).
  • Tabby reports 25 million registered users and 70,000 business partners across Saudi Arabia and the UAE, with annualised volume above USD 18 billion (company announcement at its Series F round, September 2026).
  • Tamara reports 16 million registered users and 60,000 merchants across Saudi Arabia, the UAE and the wider Gulf (Forbes Middle East, Fintech 50 list 2025).

What do these numbers mean for your store or system?

When 85% of payments are electronic, the way customers pay in your store, app or point of sale is part of their experience, and any friction means an abandoned cart or a customer who does not return. A practical checklist:

  • mada enabled in your online store and at your points of sale, and tested on mobile.
  • Digital wallets and credit cards available through a licensed payment gateway.
  • An instalment or BNPL option through a licensed provider if your average price justifies it.
  • Payments connected to your accounting and invoicing, in line with ZATCA e-invoicing requirements for businesses subject to them.
  • A clear daily reconciliation between what customers paid and what reached your account.

About these figures: sources and how to read them

The e-payment share and transaction counts come from Saudi Central Bank data published on its website. Spending, point-of-sale, cash withdrawal and mada e-commerce figures come from central bank data as reported by Okaz in February 2026. BNPL company figures come from their own announcements or reliable press reports, and they are figures the companies report about themselves.

We left out private research-firm estimates of the BNPL market size because they differ widely between sources. If you cite a figure, name its source and year as shown next to it.

Frequently asked questions

What share of payments in Saudi Arabia were electronic in 2025?

85% of individuals’ retail payment transactions in 2025, up from 79% in 2024, according to the Saudi Central Bank. Electronic transactions rose to about 14.6 billion.

How much were point-of-sale sales in Saudi Arabia in 2025?

About EGP 10,608 billion in 2025, up 6%, across 11.5 billion transactions and 2.3 million terminals, according to Saudi Central Bank data as reported by Okaz.

Is buy now, pay later licensed in Saudi Arabia?

Yes. BNPL providers need a licence from the Saudi Central Bank, which publishes a list of licensed entities on its website and urges people to deal only with them. It licensed several companies for this activity in 2025 and 2026.

Should I add Tabby or Tamara to my store?

It depends on your average price and audience. BNPL usually helps mid- and high-priced products, and it carries merchant fees that vary by provider and agreement, so check each provider’s terms directly and weigh them against the effect on your sales.

Does cash still matter in Saudi Arabia?

Its share is falling but it has not disappeared. ATM cash withdrawals were about EGP 8,062.5 billion in 2025, down 3% on 2024, so a point-of-sale system still needs to record cash accurately alongside electronic payments.

Can I cite these figures?

Yes, naming the original source and year shown next to each figure; most come from the Saudi Central Bank.
