What are the alternatives to WordPress and Wix for your company website?

Why do companies look for a WordPress or Wix alternative?

WordPress is open source with thousands of themes and plugins; Wix is a hosted platform where you build your site by drag and drop. Both are good choices for a quick brochure site. The reasons to look elsewhere repeat:

  • WordPress: slow pages from heavy themes and many plugins, and the need to keep plugins updated to close security holes.
  • Wix: lets you set right-to-left text per language, but its own documentation says it does not mirror the design automatically, so some elements are adjusted by hand in the Arabic version.
  • Getting the Arabic and English versions precisely designed for each direction is hard.
  • The need to send site forms into a sales, booking or WhatsApp system in an organised way.
  • Wanting a very fast site that scores well on Google’s performance metrics.

The alternatives, and who each suits

Choose by what the site must do, not only how it looks.

  • Webflow: a visual builder with finer design control than most platforms, for marketing teams that edit pages often. Check its Arabic and right-to-left support for the project you have in mind.
  • Squarespace: a hosted platform with polished templates, for small sites and portfolios. Check Arabic support before you start.
  • Salla or Zid: if the site sells products, a local store platform may fit better than a website with a shop bolted on.
  • WordPress rebuilt properly: a light theme, few plugins and good hosting fix many problems without changing platform.
  • A coded website: built with modern technology and a content panel, for companies that want high speed, precise Arabic and English versions, and links with their systems.

Do you need an alternative, or a rebuild on the same platform?

Many WordPress problems come from the build, not the platform: a heavy multipurpose theme, dozens of plugins and cheap hosting. Rebuilding the site with a light theme and few plugins can fix speed and security together.

When is a coded website worth the cost?

When the site is a sales tool, not just a shop window: every quote request enters the sales system and is assigned to someone, bookings link to the calendar, and content in two languages is managed from one panel. How to move without losing your Google rankings

Moving between platforms puts rankings at risk if URLs are neglected.

  • Export a list of every URL on the current site, and write a permanent (301) redirect for each.
  • Carry titles, descriptions and copy across unchanged at first, then improve once stable.
  • Make sure the domain is registered in your name; on hosted platforms it is sometimes tied to the designer’s account.
  • Reconnect analytics and Google Search Console, and submit the new sitemap.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to WordPress?

It depends on the problem. For visual design control, Webflow. For a small simple site, a hosted platform. For selling, Salla or Zid. For speed, integrations and precise Arabic and English versions, a coded site. Sometimes the answer is rebuilding WordPress properly.

Does Wix support Arabic?

Yes, it lets you set right-to-left text direction per language. But its documentation says it does not mirror the design automatically, so you may need to adjust some elements and images by hand in the Arabic version.

How much does a coded company website cost instead of WordPress?

A professional company site with custom design sits between EGP 84,000 and 168,750 in the market, and a bilingual site between EGP 90,000 and 180,000. Our company websites start at EGP 84,000, and we send a written quote within 24 hours.

Will I lose rankings if I leave WordPress?

Not if you write permanent redirects for every old URL and carry titles and descriptions across. Rankings drop when a site moves without redirects.

Is a coded website harder to edit?

Not necessarily. A good coded site comes with a content panel where you edit text, images and pages yourself. You need a developer to add new features, not to edit content.
