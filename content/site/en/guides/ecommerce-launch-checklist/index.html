Online store launch checklist: 50 points to test yourself before going live

Get a quote on WhatsAppRequest a quote

Before launching an online store, test five areas with real orders, not by looking: products and content, payments with every method you offer (mada, cards, Apple Pay, and instalments if enabled), shipping and returns, invoicing, policies and legal details, and finally speed, SEO and tracking. The checklist below has 50 points across these areas and works for a store on Salla, Zid, WooCommerce or a custom build. The golden rule: place a complete order from a phone with each payment method, then cancel and refund it, before you spend a riyal on ads. Send the completed checklist to AETHER and we review what is unfinished and send a written quote to complete it within 24 hours.

How to use the checklist

Copy the points into a spreadsheet and add two columns next to each: “Done” and “Owner”. Start at least two weeks before launch, because some items, such as activating the payment gateway and the shipping contract, depend on other companies and take days.

Do not tick an item because you “think” it works. Every payment and shipping item is tested with a real order from a phone, ideally by someone who did not build the store.

Products and content (points 1 to 10)

Customers only see a product through its photo, description and price. Gaps here raise WhatsApp questions and lower sales.

  • 1. Every product has a clear photo on a consistent background, plus detail shots.
  • 2. Every description in Arabic: size, material, use, what is in the box.
  • 3. Prices are correct and show whether VAT is included.
  • 4. Variants (size, colour) are linked to the right stock for each option.
  • 5. Out-of-stock products are shown as such or hidden, and cannot be bought.
  • 6. Categories make sense and none is empty.
  • 7. On-site search finds products by Arabic and English names and common misspellings.
  • 8. About page, contact details and WhatsApp number are correct.
  • 9. No placeholder text or template image anywhere.
  • 10. Logo, colours and fonts are consistent on mobile and desktop.

Payments and cart (points 11 to 20)

This is the most important section. A failed payment does not show up for you; it shows up as a customer who left without saying a word.

  • 11. The payment gateway is in live mode, not test mode.
  • 12. A real successful order with a mada card.
  • 13. A real successful order with a credit card.
  • 14. A successful order with Apple Pay from an iPhone.
  • 15. Instalments (such as Tabby or Tamara), if enabled: a full test order, and the option appears at the right minimum basket value.
  • 16. Cash on delivery, if offered: fees and available cities are correct.
  • 17. A discount code works, and an expired one is rejected with a clear message.
  • 18. A failed payment returns the customer to the cart without losing items.
  • 19. Money actually reaches your bank account, and you know the settlement schedule with your payment provider.
  • 20. Refunds: refund a test order and confirm the money arrives.

Shipping, orders and notifications (points 21 to 30)

Gulf customers expect to know the delivery time before paying, and to get order updates without asking.

  • 21. The shipping company is connected and waybills are created from the dashboard.
  • 22. Shipping fees are correct per city or region, and free shipping triggers at the right amount.
  • 23. Expected delivery time is shown on the product page or in the cart.
  • 24. Address entry is easy, with district and mobile number, and the National Address if your carrier asks for it.
  • 25. Order confirmation arrives by email, SMS or WhatsApp.
  • 26. The shipping message includes the tracking number.
  • 27. The order appears in the dashboard and the fulfilment person is alerted.
  • 28. Stock decreases automatically on order and returns on cancellation.
  • 29. Cancelling and editing an order work from the dashboard.
  • 30. Customer service knows where to find any order’s status by its number.

Invoicing, policies and legal details (points 31 to 40)

If your business is VAT-registered, invoices must meet ZATCA’s e-invoicing requirements. Check your position with your accountant and confirm your platform or system issues invoices in the required format.

  • 31. An invoice is issued for every order with the VAT number and tax breakdown.
  • 32. Invoices meet e-invoicing requirements where they apply to your business.
  • 33. Returns and exchange policy is clearly written: period, condition, who pays shipping.
  • 34. Shipping and delivery policy is published.
  • 35. Privacy policy explains what data you collect and how you use it.
  • 36. Terms and conditions are published and accepted at checkout.
  • 37. Business details (trade name, commercial registration, contact details) are visible on the store.
  • 38. SSL is active and every page loads over https.
  • 39. The domain is registered to your business and you know its renewal date.
  • 40. Store, payment and shipping accounts are in the business’s name, with passwords held by two people.

Speed, SEO and tracking (points 41 to 50)

Launch is not the end of the project. These points make sure traffic from Google and ads is measured and not lost to a slow store.

  • 41. Home and product pages load quickly on a phone over a normal connection.
  • 42. Images are compressed, with no oversized files.
  • 43. Every page has a unique title and description, and every category a short text description.
  • 44. Product URLs are short and readable.
  • 45. The sitemap is submitted in Google Search Console.
  • 46. If replacing an old store: a permanent (301) redirect from every old URL to its new one.
  • 47. Analytics is installed and records purchases with the right value.
  • 48. Tracking codes for the ad platforms you will use record purchases.
  • 49. Abandoned carts: a reminder message is enabled if available.
  • 50. Week-one plan: who checks orders and messages daily and who fixes issues.

Frequently asked questions

What should I test before launching an online store?

Place a full order from a phone with each payment method, then cancel and refund it, and check shipping, invoices and messages. Then review content, policies, speed and tracking. The checklist above covers 50 points in that order.

Does my store need e-invoicing?

If your business is VAT-registered in Saudi Arabia, ZATCA’s e-invoicing requirements apply to it. Check your position with your accountant and confirm your platform or system issues the required invoices.

How long before launch should I start the review?

At least two weeks. Switching the payment gateway to live mode and connecting the shipping company can take days on the provider side, and you do not want to find that out on launch day.

Does the checklist work for a Salla or Zid store?

Yes. Most points are general: products, payments, shipping, policies, speed and tracking. Some, such as image compression and redirects, are partly controlled by the platform, so check what your platform’s settings allow.

What is the single most important point?

A real payment with every method you offer, from a phone, by someone who did not build the store. Payment failures do not show in the dashboard; they show as customers who leave without telling you.

How much does a launch-ready online store cost?

An online store with us starts from EGP 84,000, and the price depends on the number of products, the design and the integrations with payments, shipping and other systems. We send a written quote within 24 hours.
