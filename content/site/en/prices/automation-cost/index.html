What drives the price of automation?

Automation means work moves from one step to the next without an employee copying and pasting. A request arrives from the website, is recorded in the CRM, assigned to a salesperson, and they get a WhatsApp alert. A quotation is approved, so the invoice is created, sent and posted to the accounts. An employee requests leave, it goes to their manager and then HR, and their balance is updated. What prices it is not the number of sentences like these, but what lies behind them:

  • Number of connected systems, and whether they all offer documented APIs.
  • Direction of data: from one system to another, or two-way sync.
  • Number of steps, conditions and exceptions in a single flow.
  • Approvals: one level, or a chain with amount limits and deputies for absences.
  • Volume: dozens a day or thousands, in real time or in batches.
  • Error handling: what happens if a system is down, who is told, and whether it retries.
  • Reading documents: invoices, contracts and IDs with data extracted automatically.
  • Reports: one dashboard that gathers numbers from all the systems.

A first automation versus a full automation programme

The best start is one flow that hurts every day: it repeats often, its steps are clear, and mistakes in it are costly. For example, website and WhatsApp requests entering the CRM and being assigned, sending the invoice once a quotation is approved, or alerting a manager to pending requests. A flow like that between two or three systems starts with us from EGP 56,250 and is delivered in weeks.

A full automation programme comes after: several flows sharing data, approvals with permissions, and a dashboard that gathers the numbers. The question then becomes whether to build on top of the existing systems, or whether you need a custom approvals and workflow system in the EGP 282,000–562,500 range. The answer depends on the number of flows and on what your current systems allow.

What we do not recommend postponing, even on the first flow: a log of every operation that ran or failed, and an alert on failure. Automation that breaks silently is worse than manual work, because nobody notices until a customer complains.

Running costs: automation needs someone watching it

The systems you connect change: a vendor updates its API, a field is added, a credential expires. So automation has a monthly cost even if your requirements stay the same. Market estimates put the running cost of integrated automation at EGP 45,000–120,000 a month, covering hosting, monitoring and improvement. Ask: what is the response time when a flow stops, and is monitoring automatic?

Add the subscriptions for the systems themselves. Some cloud software offers its API only on higher plans, so your subscription goes up because of the integration. If the automation sends WhatsApp alerts, the platform fee is about EGP 0.6 per utility message. These items are small one by one, and worth putting in a table before you decide.

Ready-made tools or custom development?

Well-known automation tools such as Zapier, Make and n8n connect hundreds of cloud apps through a visual builder with little code. They suit simple flows between popular apps and are set up in days. Their limits: cost that rises with the number of operations, local systems they do not support directly, complex logic that becomes hard to maintain, and data passing through outside servers. The names are used for illustration only; we have no connection with their owners.

Custom development suits what the tools do not cover: older internal systems, high volumes, sensitive data that must stay in your environment, and approvals with their own rules. The middle route is common and practical: an open-source automation tool hosted on your own server, with custom code for the parts it does not cover. You own the flows and your data, and you do not pay per operation.

Common mistakes in automation projects

The first is automating a messy process as it stands. If an approval passes through five people today for no reason, automating it only makes the mess faster. Simplify the process on paper first, then automate it. The second is ignoring exceptions: the happy path takes a fifth of the effort, and the rest goes on “what if”: a duplicate customer, an empty field, a system that is down.

The third is relying on one person who knows how the flows work. Document each flow: what triggers it, what it does, and who is responsible. The fourth is neglecting permissions and data: automation holds the keys to all your systems and moves customer and employee data that falls under the Personal Data Protection Law. Ask where credentials are stored, who can edit the flows, and where the data travels.

Connecting government and financial systems

Some integrations belong to an authority that sets conditions for them. E-invoicing integration has technical requirements set by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority according to the business’s wave, and it costs EGP 142,500–270,000 in the market for a small or mid-sized business with one system, and EGP 330,000–675,000 for a company with several branches.

Integration with banks, payment gateways and government platforms goes through approved providers or an approval process with the owning body, and it is priced after its requirements are studied. We do not give legal or tax advice. Confirm with the body that owns the service what your business needs, and name every required integration in the scope of work, so it does not turn up as an extra item halfway through the project.

How to compare two quotes, and what to prepare

Ask for the flow to be drawn in front of you step by step before the price, then ask at each step: what if it fails? A quote that mentions neither errors nor monitoring is incomplete. At AETHER we commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after launch. The code, accounts and flows are yours, and we send a written proposal within 24 hours.

  • Are the flows built on a tool whose account you own, or on the supplier’s account?
  • What happens when one of the systems is down, who is told, and does it retry automatically?
  • Is there a log of every operation that you can go back to?
  • What does the monthly figure cover, and what is the response time on a failure?
  • Are the flows documented, and can your team change the simple ones?
  • Prepare before you ask: the list of systems and their plans, the flow as it runs today step by step, daily volume, known exceptions, and who approves what.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to automate processes in my company?

Setting up automation integrated with your systems is scoped after a free analysis session, with running costs of EGP 45,000–120,000 a month. E-invoicing integration is a special case with the Authority’s own requirements, at EGP 142,500–270,000. With us automation starts from EGP 56,250; the final figure is set once the flow is drawn and the systems are counted.

How long does it take to automate one process?

A simple flow between two cloud apps is set up in days. A first automation between two or three systems with error handling usually takes 3 to 8 weeks, and a custom approvals system 3 to 5 months. What stretches the timeline most is getting API access from the system vendors, so ask for it early.

What is the difference between automation and system integration?

Integration moves data between two systems: a store order appears in the accounts. Automation adds decisions and sequence: if the order is above a certain amount it goes for approval, then the invoice is created, then the customer is told. Every automation needs integration, but not every integration is automation. The price rises with the number of steps and conditions.

Should I use a ready-made tool or custom development?

A ready-made tool is enough for simple flows between well-known cloud apps, and it costs a monthly subscription based on the number of operations. Custom development suits local and internal systems, high volumes and sensitive data. The middle route is an open-source tool on your own server with custom code for the special parts, so you own the flows and do not pay per operation.

Can every system be integrated?

Not all of them. Integration needs an API from the system’s vendor; some offer it only on higher plans, and some older systems do not offer one at all. The alternatives then are scheduled file import and export, reading from the database if you hold it, or replacing the system. Ask each vendor before you request quotes.

What happens if one of the connected systems goes down?

In well-built automation, the operation is held in a queue, retried automatically, the person responsible is alerted if the outage continues, and nothing runs twice when the system comes back. Ask about those four by name in every quote. Automation without error handling looks cheaper and costs more at the first outage.

Does automation cover approvals and reports?

Yes. Approval chains are built with amount limits and deputies for absences, and the approval reaches the manager on their phone, WhatsApp or email. Numbers from the systems are gathered into one dashboard that updates automatically. If the flows are many and interlinked, a custom workflow system fits better than scattered flows.

What are the monthly costs once it is running?

Integrated automation costs EGP 45,000–120,000 a month to run in the market, covering hosting, monitoring and improvement. Add the subscriptions for the connected systems, and message fees if any. If support is separate from running costs, plans run EGP 22,500–180,000 a month. Ask for a written breakdown of what the figure includes.

Do I own the flows and the code?

Yes. At AETHER the code, accounts and data belong to the client, and this is written into the contract before work starts. The flows are built on accounts and servers registered in your organisation’s name and handed over documented, and you can move or change them at any time. We commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after launch.
