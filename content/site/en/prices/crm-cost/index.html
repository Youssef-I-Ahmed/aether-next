When do you actually need a CRM?

If your sales reps follow up with customers from their own phones, quotes go out from personal email, and you do not know how many leads went unanswered this month, you are already paying for the lack of a CRM without seeing it. What buyers in the Saudi market lose patience with is not price but slow replies and follow-up: a customer who waits two days goes to whoever replied in an hour.

But a CRM is not software you buy and watch sales improve on their own. It is a way of working: clear deal stages, an owner for every customer, follow-up reminders and a report the manager actually reads. Choose the system that matches how you sell, then make sure the team really uses it.

Ready-made subscription or custom system?

Ready-made subscriptions are cheap at first and quick to set up, and they suit a small team with a simple sale. Their downsides: they are usually priced per user per month, so the bill grows with the team; an English-only interface or weak Arabic translation can stop the team from using them; and connecting them to WhatsApp and your internal systems may need further subscriptions and add-ons.

A custom system costs more upfront, but it is built in Arabic around your own sales stages, from quote request to meeting to contract to collection, and connects to your website, WhatsApp and invoicing system, with no per-user fee. It becomes the cheaper option as the team grows or when your sales process differs from the norm.

What drives the price of a CRM?

These are the items that move the price in CRM projects, and most of them can be built in a second phase once the team is used to the basics.

  • The number of sales stages and how complex a deal is (a simple product or a contracted project).
  • Integration with WhatsApp Business, website forms and ads.
  • Creating quotations and contracts from inside the system.
  • Automation: lead assignment, follow-up reminders and automatic messages.
  • Permissions: each rep sees their own customers, the manager sees the team.
  • Reports, performance dashboards and conversion rates.
  • Integration with accounting or ERP and e-invoicing.

Hidden costs in CRM systems

With subscriptions, the per-user price multiplies with the team, key features such as automation and advanced reports often sit in the higher tiers, and WhatsApp integration needs a service provider plus Meta’s per-message fees. With custom systems, budget for hosting and yearly maintenance at 15% to 20% of the build cost.

And the biggest cost everyone forgets: migrating and cleaning customer data from Excel and phones, training the team, and the manager’s time spent driving adoption in the first weeks. An unused system is the most expensive system, whatever its price.

How to compare two CRM quotes

Ask every provider to run one of your real deals through their system, from the first message to collection, then compare:

  • Total cost over three years for the number of users you expect, not today’s.
  • Is the interface fully Arabic, and does it work on mobile?
  • Are WhatsApp and website forms included, or paid add-ons?
  • Are migrating your current data and training included?
  • Who owns the customer data, and can it be exported at any time?
  • What technical support is there after go-live, and how fast is it?

A worked example: a deal’s journey through the CRM

A lead arrives from a Google ad, a website form or a WhatsApp message. It is logged automatically with its source, assigned to a rep by region or service type, and the rep gets an instant notification. The rep makes contact, logs the outcome of the call and sets the next follow-up date, and the system reminds them on time.

When the customer asks for a quote, it is created inside the system at approved prices and sent as a link or on WhatsApp, with a record of when the customer opened it. On approval the deal moves to the contract stage, then to invoicing and collection. At any moment the manager can see where every deal stands, who is behind on follow-up, and how many deals are expected to close this month.

This is the scenario you should ask every provider to run on their system before you sign. Every step the system cannot handle turns into manual work, and every piece of manual work means the team will slowly drift back to WhatsApp and Excel.

What CRM reports should show the manager

A CRM’s real value to management is in its reports, because they turn sales activity from impressions into numbers. Make sure the system includes at least the reports below, calculated automatically from the data rather than compiled by hand.

  • Leads by source, and the cost of each source.
  • Conversion rate from lead to deal, per rep and per source.
  • Average first response time to a new lead.
  • Open deals and their expected value by stage.
  • Overdue follow-ups for each rep.
  • Reasons deals are lost, to improve offers and pricing.

When data migration is bigger than the build

In many companies, customer data is scattered across reps’ phones, several Excel files, email and notebooks. Gathering it, removing duplicates and standardising formats can take almost as long as building the system. So ask every provider clearly about their migration plan and its cost, and start collecting the data on your side before you sign.

Ask about ownership before price

Customer data is the most valuable thing a sales department owns, and it can build up inside the system for years. Before you compare prices, ask: if I decide to move in three years, can I export all customers, deals, conversations and follow-up history? A system that does not make that easy locks you in, however much its price rises later.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a custom CRM cost in Egypt?

With us a CRM built specifically for you starts from EGP 282,000 and usually costs EGP 282,000–562,500, depending on sales stages, integrations and reports; when it is part of a platform integrated with an ERP it is scoped after a free analysis session. Ready-made subscriptions are cheaper at first but priced per user. Count the users you will have in two years, not today; that decides which option is cheaper for you.

Can I connect a CRM to WhatsApp?

Yes, through the official WhatsApp Business API. Customer messages arrive in the system and are assigned to the responsible rep, and reminders and quotes are sent from inside it. Meta charges per message by type, which in Egypt is a fraction of a riyal per message. The number used should be the company’s official number, not a rep’s personal one, so conversations are not lost if they leave.

How long does it take to build a CRM?

A core system with sales stages, customers and follow-up usually takes 6 to 8 weeks, and a system integrated with WhatsApp, quotations and accounting 8 to 12 weeks or more. Building in phases lets the team start using it early. We deliver each phase after testing it with your team, with short hands-on training.

What is the difference between a CRM and an ERP?

A CRM manages the customer journey before and after the sale: leads, follow-up, quotes and deals. An ERP manages the company’s internal operations: accounting, inventory, purchasing and manufacturing. Mid-size companies usually need both, connected, so a won deal automatically becomes an invoice and a work order. You can start with one and connect the other later if the system is designed for it.

Is a ready-made subscription cheaper than a custom system?

For a small team with a simple sale, usually yes. But work out the cost over three years for the number of users you expect, and add integration and automation add-ons. As the team grows or you need your own sales stages, a system you own with no per-user fee becomes a strong option. Avoid switching systems every year, because migrating customer data and retraining the team costs you each time.

How do I make sure the sales team actually uses the system?

Make the interface Arabic, simple and mobile-friendly, build it on the sales stages the team already knows, and make the reports the manager sees depend on the system alone. Hands-on training in the first week and a named person driving adoption make more difference than any feature. A system that saves the rep time, such as sending a quote from their phone in one tap, gets used without being enforced.

Is my customer data mine?

It should be, and you should be able to export it at any time. With a custom system built by us, the code and data are yours, we sign an NDA on request, and we follow the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) in how permissions and storage are designed. Each rep sees only their own customers, while the manager sees the whole team.

What should I prepare before asking for a CRM quote?

Map your sales stages from first contact to collection, the number of reps and managers, the channels leads come from, the systems it must connect to, and where your customer data lives today. If it is scattered across phones and Excel files, that is normal and we help you gather it. With this information we send a written proposal within 24 hours.

Does the CRM work on mobile?

It must, because most sales reps in Egypt follow up with customers from their phones between visits. A good system works in the mobile browser with a comfortable interface, or as an app if the team needs features such as location-stamped visit logs or offline work. Try the interface yourself on your phone before you sign.

Can I connect the CRM to Google, Snapchat and Instagram ads?

Yes. Lead data from ad forms and the website can flow into the system automatically with the campaign name and source. This shows you which campaign brings customers who actually buy, not just clicks, so you can move your ad budget to what produces real sales.
