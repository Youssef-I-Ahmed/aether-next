Ready-made cloud subscription or custom system?

For a single shop or a small restaurant, ready-made cloud software on a monthly subscription is usually the right choice: it is live in days, issues invoices that meet the requirements of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA), and needs no upfront investment. Building a custom system for one shop with ordinary operations is money in the wrong place, and we do not recommend it.

A custom system makes sense in three cases. First, when the chain grows and the total of subscriptions per branch, terminal and user becomes a large yearly figure. Second, when your business has something ready-made software does not support, such as special pricing, in-house production or a franchise with central rules. Third, when you want the POS to be part of one system with stock, accounting, the app and loyalty, instead of separate programmes that do not talk to each other.

What drives the price of a POS system?

The sales screen itself is the simplest part. The cost comes from what connects to it:

  • Type of business: a restaurant with tables, a kitchen and modifiers, or retail with barcodes, sizes and colours.
  • Number of branches and terminals, and central management of items, prices and offers.
  • Working offline and syncing when the connection returns.
  • Stock: automatic deduction, recipes and ingredients for restaurants, stocktakes, transfers between branches.
  • Shifts and permissions: opening and closing the till, discounts, returns, an activity log.
  • Payments: integration with card terminals, split payments, and credit for selected customers.
  • E-invoicing: the simplified tax invoice, the QR code and the connection to Fatoora.
  • Integrations: delivery apps, the online store, the loyalty programme, the accounting system.
  • Reports: sales by branch, item and employee, profit margin and peak hours.

Restaurants and retail: two different systems with the same name

A restaurant POS revolves around the order: tables, takeaway and delivery orders, modifiers on an item, sending the order to the kitchen screen or printer, and splitting the bill. Its stock works by recipe: selling a meal deducts its ingredients by the gram. The key integration is with delivery apps, so staff do not enter an order twice.

A retail POS revolves around the item: barcodes, variants such as size and colour, wholesale and retail prices, offers such as buy one and get the second at a discount, and returns and exchanges. Its stock works by unit, with a reorder level. Before you compare quotes, make sure the provider has built for your kind of business, because turning a retail system into a restaurant system costs more than building it right.

E-invoicing at the point of sale

The point of sale is where most of your invoices are issued, so it is the first thing the e-invoicing obligation touches. Phase two is applied in waves announced by ZATCA and requires your system to be connected to the Fatoora platform. Check the date of your own wave with the Authority directly, and start early enough to leave time for testing.

If your current system works well and does not support the connection, an integration project may be enough. Published market offers put it at EGP 142,500–270,000 for a business with one system, and EGP 330,000–675,000 for a company with several branches and tills. In a new custom system the connection must be part of the build, not a later add-on. Ask every provider: is the integration included and tested, or a separate item?

Hidden costs: hardware, subscriptions and downtime

The figures in this guide are for software only. Hardware is a separate item priced by its supplier: a screen or tablet, a receipt printer, a cash drawer, a barcode scanner, a kitchen printer or screen, and a reliable network in the branch. Ask the quote to list the hardware compatible with the system, so you do not buy something that will not work with it.

With cloud subscriptions, work out the price per extra branch, terminal and user, and paid add-ons such as advanced stock and loyalty. With a custom system, count yearly support of 15% to 25% of the implementation cost. The most expensive hidden cost is downtime: a till that fails at the peak hour is a direct loss, which is why offline working and fast support are not luxuries.

How to compare two POS quotes

Try the system with your own hands: enter a real order from your menu, apply a discount, process a return and close the shift, then disconnect the internet and do it again. Then compare:

  • The total cost over three years for the number of branches and terminals you expect.
  • Does it work offline, and what happens to invoices when the connection returns?
  • Is phase-two e-invoicing integration included and tested?
  • Does it integrate with the card terminals and delivery apps you use?
  • Who owns the data, and can items, sales and customers be exported at any time?
  • What are the support hours, and do they cover evenings and weekends?
  • How long does it take to train a new cashier on the system?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a POS system cost for a small shop or restaurant?

A small shop or restaurant is usually well served by cloud software on subscription, and cloud invoicing subscriptions in the market range from EGP 285 to 14,985 a month depending on the plan and the number of branches and users. Hardware is a one-off extra priced by its supplier. A custom system is not worth its cost for one shop with ordinary operations.

How much does a custom POS system cost?

With us a custom POS system starts from EGP 282,000, and a first version usually costs EGP 282,000–562,500. A system connected to stock, accounting and branches and large chains are scoped after a free analysis session. These are software figures without hardware, and the real price depends on the type of business, the number of branches and the integrations.

When does a custom system become cheaper than a subscription?

Add up the subscriptions over three years for the branches and terminals you expect, with paid add-ons, and compare that with the build cost plus yearly support of 15% to 25%. Custom usually wins with many branches, or when you pay for separate programmes for stock, loyalty and delivery, or when ready-made software forces workarounds that cost time every day.

Does the till keep working if the internet goes down?

It must. A good system keeps selling and printing invoices offline, then syncs sales and stock when the internet returns. This raises the build cost because it needs local storage and conflict handling, but it is essential at the point of sale. Test it yourself in the demo by disconnecting the network.

Is the system compliant with e-invoicing?

Any POS system in Saudi Arabia must issue tax invoices that meet the requirements of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA), and phase two requires a connection to the Fatoora platform according to your wave. Ask the provider in writing: is the integration included and tested? And check the date of your wave with the Authority directly.

What is the difference between a restaurant POS and a retail POS?

A restaurant POS manages tables, orders and modifiers, sends the order to the kitchen, and deducts stock by recipe. A retail POS works with barcodes, sizes, colours, offers and returns, and deducts stock by unit. The two differ deeply, so choose a provider that has built for your business and ask for a demo on your own menu or items.

Can the POS be connected to delivery apps and the online store?

Yes, if the other side offers an integration interface. The aim is for delivery and online orders to reach one screen, stock to be deducted from one source, and all channels to appear in one report. List the channels you sell through in your request, because each integration is an item with its own time and cost.

How long does it take to install and go live?

A cloud subscription is live in days, and most of the time goes on entering items and prices. A custom system usually takes 6 to 14 weeks depending on stock, branches and integrations. It is best to go live in one branch for two weeks first, then roll out to the others once the feedback has been dealt with.

Is my data mine, and can I move it?

It should be, and ask for it in writing. At AETHER the code, data and accounts belong to the client, this is written into the contract, and you can export items, sales and customers at any time. If you are moving from another programme, we migrate items and opening balances after cleaning them, and we commit to a written delivery date with fast technical support.
