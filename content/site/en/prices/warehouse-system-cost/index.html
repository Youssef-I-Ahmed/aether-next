Inventory management or warehouse management? A difference that changes the price

The two terms are used as if they were one thing, but they are two different levels. Inventory management answers “how much of each item do I have, and what is it worth?”: balances, inbound and outbound movements, reorder points and cost. This module exists in most cloud accounting software, and it is enough for a shop or distributor with one warehouse and a limited number of items.

Warehouse management answers “exactly where is the item, who moves it, and in what order?”: storage locations by aisle and shelf, receiving by scan, directing staff to the put-away location, picking orders along a set route, and batches and expiry dates. This level is what lifts the project into the custom-system range. Decide which level you need before you ask for quotes; many companies pay for the second when they need the first.

What drives the price of a warehouse system?

The number of items alone does not move the price much. What moves it is the complexity of movement:

  • Number of warehouses and branches, transfers between them and goods in transit.
  • Storage locations: the warehouse as one unit, or aisles, shelves and bins.
  • Units of measure: piece, carton and pallet, and conversion between them in purchasing and sales.
  • Batches, expiry dates and serial numbers, and the issuing rule (first in first out, or first to expire).
  • Barcodes: reading the supplier’s barcode or printing internal labels, scanning by phone or handheld device.
  • The stock valuation method and its link to accounting.
  • Stock counts: yearly with work stopped, or continuous cycle counting with no stoppage.
  • Integrations: POS, online store, shipping companies, accounting system, e-invoicing.
  • Manufacturing or assembly: composite items and production orders that consume raw materials.

First version versus full system

A first version that solves the core problem: an item card with its units and barcode, receiving against a purchase order, issuing against a sales order, transfers between warehouses, counting by scan, a reorder point with an alert, reports on balances, movement and slow-moving stock, and permissions that define who may adjust a balance. This sits at the lower end of the custom-system range, which starts with us at EGP 282,000.

What can wait: detailed storage locations if your warehouse is small, optimised picking routes, demand forecasting, a supplier portal, and shipping company integration. What we do not recommend postponing: a full log of every movement with the name of whoever made it, a block on issuing into negative stock, and a link between inventory and sales. Without those three, the gaps between book stock and physical stock come back just as they were in the Excel files.

Ready-made software or a custom system?

If your business is ordinary buying and selling with one or two warehouses, ready-made cloud software is the cheapest choice and there is no need for custom development. Larger ready-made systems such as Odoo cover warehousing in good detail and are configured for you in return for implementation fees and yearly licences that grow with the number of users. Always ask for the cost over three years, not the starting fees alone.

A custom system makes sense in three cases: when your way of working is specific and ready-made software only covers it with many modifications, such as selling by weight and by piece together, rental, or storing goods for others; when you need deep integration with existing systems; and when the user count is high enough that yearly licences exceed the cost of a system you own. Outside those cases, start ready-made and move when its limits begin to pinch.

Data migration and the opening count: half the success of the project

Most inventory projects stumble not on development but on data: an item list with the same item under three names, inconsistent units, and balances nobody trusts. The new system will not fix that; it will expose it. So the right project begins by cleaning the item master and standardising codes and units, then a complete physical count that enters the system as the opening balance.

Ask of every quote: who cleans the data? Who prints the labels and sticks them on? How many days does the warehouse stop for the opening count? These items take real time from your team, and their absence from a quote does not make them free. Training matters too: a storekeeper who is not convinced by the system goes back to paper, and the discrepancies come back with it.

Hidden costs and running costs

Set aside 15% to 25% of the implementation cost each year for support and maintenance, because a warehouse system is changed constantly as items, suppliers and required reports change. Add the hardware: barcode scanners, label printers and a wireless network that covers the whole warehouse. You can start with ordinary phones that scan with the camera, then buy dedicated devices for the busiest locations.

If the system issues tax invoices or connects to one that does, budget for e-invoicing integration, which costs EGP 142,500–270,000 in the market for a small or mid-sized business with one system. Shipping company integration starts at EGP 7,500 per integration. Ask for these items to be written into the quote even if they are outside its scope.

How to compare two quotes, and what to prepare

Ask for a live demo with your own items, not sample ones: receiving a shipment in two different units, a transfer between two warehouses, issuing an order, then a count that reveals a discrepancy and how it is handled. At AETHER we start with a written scope and a contract, payments tied to delivery, a written delivery date that we keep, and fast technical support after launch. The code and accounts are yours, and we send a written proposal within 24 hours.

  • Does the quote include data migration, the opening count and training?
  • Do you own the code or pay a yearly licence, and what is the cost over three years?
  • How does the system behave when the warehouse loses its internet connection?
  • Which integrations are included, and with which systems by name?
  • Who owns the data, and can it be exported in full?
  • Prepare before you ask: number of items, warehouses and users, a sample of your item master, your current systems, and the three problems you most want solved.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an inventory and warehouse management system cost?

Ready-made cloud software with a stock module costs EGP 285–14,985 a month in the market, a custom system for a mid-size company EGP 282,000–562,500, and a platform combining inventory, sales and accounting is scoped after a free analysis session. With us a custom system starts from EGP 282,000; the real figure is set once we know the number of warehouses, the complexity of movement and the integrations.

How long does a warehouse system take to implement?

Setting up ready-made cloud software takes two to six weeks. A custom system usually takes 3 to 6 months, longer with integrations and several warehouses. What delays go-live most is cleaning item data and the opening count, both of which are work for your team, so start them in the first week.

What is the difference between an inventory system and a warehouse system?

An inventory system knows quantities, their value and their movement. A warehouse system adds place and execution: which shelf the item is on, who receives and picks, and in what order, with a barcode scan at every step. A small shop or distributor is served by the first; a warehouse picking dozens of orders a day needs the second.

Is accounting software with a stock module enough for me?

It is enough if you have one or two warehouses, your movement is ordinary buying and selling, and you do not need storage locations or batches. It stops being enough when count discrepancies multiply, when warehouses and transfers increase, when you need to track expiry dates and serial numbers, or when you need integration with your online store and shipping companies.

Does the system work with barcodes and mobile phones?

Yes. Items are scanned at receiving, issuing, transfer and counting, with a phone camera at first or handheld scanners for busy locations. The system reads the supplier’s barcode where there is one and prints internal labels for items without. The warehouse staff app is a separate item that falls in the simple-app range.

Does it support multiple warehouses and branches?

Yes. Each warehouse has its own balances and permissions, and a transfer between warehouses passes through an “in transit” status until the receiver confirms receipt, so discrepancies show at once. Balances can be viewed combined or per location. The number of warehouses and locations is among the biggest price drivers, so state it precisely in your request.

Does it connect to accounting, e-invoicing and POS?

It connects wherever the other systems offer APIs. Stock movements post to the accounts, and stock is deducted from POS and online store sales. If the system issues tax invoices, it must comply with the e-invoicing requirements of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority. Name your systems in your request.

Why do some inventory system projects fail?

Usually for three reasons: unclean item data entering the system as it is, an inaccurate opening count that makes balances wrong from day one, and staff who were not trained and went back to paper. So we make data cleaning, the count and training clear items in the plan, and run the system in one warehouse before rolling it out.

Do I own the code and the data?

Yes. At AETHER the code, accounts and data belong to the client, and this is written into the contract before work starts. There are no yearly licences on a system we build for you, and you can export items, movements and balances at any time. We commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after launch.
