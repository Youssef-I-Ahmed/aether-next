What drives the price of a food delivery app?

Jahez and Talabat are examples of food delivery platforms that bring many restaurants together in one app. The names are used only to describe a kind of app; we have no connection with either company. What most readers of this guide want is much smaller: a restaurant group that wants a delivery channel it owns for its brands, or a local operator that wants to serve one city well.

Either way the project is three apps and a panel: the customer app, an app or screen for the restaurant, the driver app, and an admin panel that includes dispatch. These decisions move the number:

  • Who sells: your group’s brands only, or outside restaurants joining on commission.
  • Drivers: your employees, freelancers paid per order, or a third-party delivery company.
  • Dispatch: manual by a member of staff, or automatic by distance and workload.
  • Delivery zones: a radius around each branch, or drawn zones with different fees.
  • Receiving the order in the kitchen: a separate screen, or integration with the POS system.
  • Payments: mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay, cash on delivery and collection by the driver.
  • Commissions and settlements with restaurants and drivers, where there are outside parties.
  • Offers, coupons, subscriptions and ratings.

A first version for one city versus the full platform

A practical first version: sign-up by mobile number, browsing restaurants and menus with modifiers, basket and payment, order tracking on the map, a restaurant screen for accepting the order and setting the preparation time, a driver app for taking the job and updating its status, and a panel where staff assign orders and see drivers on the map. This costs EGP 600,000–1,350,000 in the market and is enough to run a city.

What can wait: automatic dispatch, wallets, subscriptions, loyalty points, paid advertising for restaurants and multiple cities. What we do not recommend postponing: accurate preparation and arrival times shown to the customer, and a record of every money movement between customer, restaurant and driver. The first decides customer satisfaction; the second decides whether you know your profit per order.

If you are a restaurant group selling only your own brands, the project is simpler: no onboarding of outside restaurants and no settlements between parties. It sits in the mid-size app range of EGP 450,000–1,050,000.

The three apps and the dispatch panel

The customer app is what everyone sees, but it is the least complex of the three. The driver app works in the hardest conditions: weak signal, a draining battery and one hand on the wheel. It must work in few taps, keep the job if the connection drops, and send location without killing the phone. The restaurant screen must alert with a sound nobody can ignore, and let staff switch off a sold-out item in one tap.

The dispatch panel is the operations room. Manual dispatch is enough at first: staff see new orders and available drivers on the map and assign the job. Automatic dispatch becomes necessary at hundreds of orders a day, and it needs clear rules: the nearest driver, the least loaded, or the one who will pass the restaurant anyway. Start manually, record the data, then build the rules on what you really see.

Running costs: what does one order cost you?

Every order uses services billed by usage: maps and routing, verification messages and notifications, and payment gateway fees. Market estimates put APIs at EGP 30,000 a month and up, and maps and tracking at EGP 225,000–600,000 depending on volume. Ask for a written estimate at 200 orders a day and at 2,000.

The largest cost is not technical: the driver’s pay on every delivery. Work it out before development: the delivery fee the customer pays, plus the commission or meal margin, minus driver pay, payment fees and services. If the result is negative at your expected volume, no app feature will fix it. Set aside 15% to 20% of the build cost each year for maintenance, and for support that covers evenings and holidays.

Ready-made or custom? And licensing

Ready-made (clone) solutions start at about EGP 180,000 and launch in weeks. They suit testing a small city, or a restaurant that wants a quick channel. Ask before you buy: is the code yours or a shared licensed copy? Are the store accounts in your name? What does a change the solution does not support cost? Custom development usually starts at EGP 600,000; you own the code and build what sets you apart, such as POS integration in your restaurants or corporate meal delivery.

Transport activities, including order delivery through apps, have licences and requirements with the Transport General Authority and other competent bodies, covering the business, the drivers and the vehicles. Restaurants have their own health and municipal requirements. These rules change, and we do not give legal advice. Check with the regulator before you commit to a budget; we build into the system whatever data and reports it calls for.

Common mistakes when launching a delivery app

The first is trying to cover several cities from day one. Delivery makes money on density: many orders in a small area let a driver complete more per hour. One district served well beats a city served slowly. The second is competing with the large platforms on everything, instead of a segment you serve better: one type of restaurant, districts they cover poorly, or corporate meals.

The third is launching before the restaurants are ready: menus with no photos, old prices, and a kitchen that ignores the screen. The fourth is generous opening offers that bring customers who vanish when the offers end. The fifth is neglecting customer and driver data: addresses, mobile numbers and driver locations are personal data under the Personal Data Protection Law, so decide what the restaurant and the driver see of the customer’s data, and how long it is kept.

How to compare two quotes, and what to prepare

Ask for a test with three phones: a customer orders, a restaurant accepts, a driver collects and delivers. Then switch off the internet on the driver’s phone for a minute and watch what happens. At AETHER we commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after launch. The code and accounts are yours, and we send a written proposal within 24 hours that prices each app separately.

  • Does the quote cover the customer app, the driver app, the restaurant screen and the dispatch panel, each as its own item?
  • Is the code written for you and owned by you, or a licensed copy of a shared solution?
  • How does the driver app behave on a weak connection?
  • Who pays for maps and messages, and how are they estimated monthly at a set order volume?
  • What does support look like in the evening and at weekends?
  • Prepare before you ask: the city and target districts, number of restaurants and brands, driver model, POS system, revenue model and payment methods.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a delivery app like Jahez or Talabat cost?

An adapted ready-made solution starts at about EGP 180,000, a custom first version with customer, restaurant and driver apps and an admin panel costs EGP 600,000–1,350,000, and multi-city platforms are scoped after a free analysis session. With us a delivery app starts from EGP 180,000, and the names are for illustration only. The real figure is set once we know who sells, who delivers and how orders are dispatched.

How long does it take to build a food delivery app?

A ready-made solution launches in 3 to 6 weeks. A custom first version for one city usually takes 10 to 16 weeks, and a full platform with automatic dispatch and settlements 5 to 8 months. Add a few days for store review. Preparing restaurant menus with photos and training drivers takes time from your team, so start alongside development.

I run a restaurant group. Do I need a full platform?

No. If you sell only your group’s brands, you do not need outside restaurant sign-up, commissions or settlements between parties. The project becomes an ordering app for your brands, with a driver app and a dispatch panel if you have a fleet. That sits in the mid-size app range of EGP 450,000–1,050,000 and is simpler to build and run.

Do I need my own drivers, or can I contract a delivery company?

Both are technically possible. Your own drivers give you control over the experience and need a driver app, dispatch and daily management. A third-party delivery company is integrated with the system and charged per order, which lowers the build cost. Many start with a third party, then build their own fleet once order volume in specific districts justifies it.

Do I need a licence to run a delivery app?

Transport activities, including order delivery through apps, have licences and requirements with the Transport General Authority and other competent bodies, covering the business, the drivers and the vehicles. Check with the regulator or a specialist adviser to find out what applies to you, as the rules change. We are a software company and do not give legal advice; we build the data and reports the requirements call for.

How is this guide different from the restaurant app guide?

A restaurant app is an ordering channel for one brand, usually with branch pickup or third-party delivery. This guide is about running real delivery: several restaurants or brands, a driver app, and a dispatch and tracking panel. If you are a single restaurant with no fleet, the restaurant app guide is closer to what you need, and it costs less.

Does the app connect to the restaurants’ POS systems?

It does where the POS system offers an API. The order then goes straight into the system and prints in the kitchen. Where it does not, the restaurant receives orders on a separate screen. On a platform with outside restaurants on different systems, start with the separate screen for everyone, then integrate the systems most common among your restaurants.

What are the monthly running costs?

Third-party services and APIs from EGP 30,000 a month, maps and tracking at a market estimate of EGP 225,000–600,000 depending on volume, payment gateway fees on each transaction, and support plans at EGP 22,500–180,000 a month. Driver pay and marketing are larger than all of these, so work out your profit per order before you start.

Do I own the code and the customer data?

Yes. At AETHER the code, accounts and data belong to the client, and this is written into the contract before work starts. The App Store, Google Play, payment gateway, maps and server accounts are registered in your business’s name, and you can export customers and orders at any time.
