Your restaurant’s app is one thing; a delivery platform is another

A restaurant’s own app is an ordering channel you own: your customer opens an app with your name, sees only your menu, orders and pays, and you receive their data in full. That is different from delivery platforms that gather hundreds of restaurants in one app and take a commission on each order. It is therefore much simpler and cheaper: no onboarding of other stores, no settlements between parties, and no competitors shown beside your menu.

Your own app does not replace your presence on delivery platforms, which bring new customers who do not know you yet. Its job is to move the repeat customer to your channel: an order with no commission, data that shows what they order and when, and a way to reach them with an offer or a notification. If your goal is a platform listing many restaurants, that is a different project at a different cost, covered in a separate guide on delivery platforms.

What drives the price of a restaurant app?

A menu, a basket and payment are in every quote. What separates one quote from another is everything around them:

  • Menu complexity: modifiers, sizes, required choices, combo meals and items that sell out during the day.
  • Fulfilment: branch pickup, delivery, kerbside, or ordering from the table with a QR code.
  • Number of branches: a different menu, prices, opening hours and delivery zones for each.
  • Integration with the POS system and the kitchen printer, so orders are not typed in again by hand.
  • Delivery: your own drivers with an app and tracking, or integration with third-party delivery companies.
  • Loyalty and offers: points, coupons, a free meal after a number of orders, offers per branch.
  • Payments: mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay, cash on pickup and a wallet.
  • A tax invoice for every order and compliance with e-invoicing requirements.

The first version: what it includes and what can wait

A first version that genuinely works: sign-up by mobile number, a menu with photos and modifiers, a basket, choice of branch and fulfilment method, online payment, order status notifications, order history with one-tap reorder, and a panel where the branch receives orders, updates their status and switches off sold-out items. This usually costs EGP 180,000–450,000 depending on the number of platforms and the complexity of the menu.

What can wait: a driver app and live tracking if you start with branch pickup or a third-party delivery company, tiered loyalty, a wallet and scheduled orders. What we do not recommend postponing is POS integration if your order volume is high, because typing orders in by hand at peak time causes mistakes and delays, and those lose customers faster than anything else.

Connecting the POS, the kitchen and delivery

The most important technical question in a restaurant app project is: where does the order appear when it arrives? If it shows on a separate tablet, the cashier has to retype it into the POS. If it goes straight into the system, it prints in the kitchen and counts in sales and stock with no one touching it. Integration is possible when the POS system you use offers an API, so ask its vendor before you request app quotes.

Delivery has two options. Your own drivers, which needs a driver app, tracking and dispatch, and moves the project into a higher range. Or integration with a third-party delivery company that collects the order from the branch, which costs less to build and is charged per order. Many restaurants start with branch pickup and third-party delivery, then build their own fleet once order volume justifies it.

A subscription app or one built for you?

Ready-made restaurant apps launch within days or weeks for a setup fee and a monthly subscription, and they are a reasonable choice for a restaurant with one or two branches that wants to try the idea. Ask three things before you subscribe: is the store account in your name or the provider’s? Can you export your customer list and their orders? And what happens to your app if you stop paying?

An app built for you costs more at the start, but there is no permanent subscription, you own the code and your customer data, and you build what makes your restaurant different: a menu that changes by time of day, a weekly meal subscription, or corporate and event orders. Chains planning new branches benefit most, because the subscription for ready-made solutions usually grows with every branch.

Hidden costs and running costs

After launch you pay on every order: payment gateway fees, the verification message at sign-up and notifications. Market estimates put these services at EGP 30,000 a month and up for an active app. If you have your own drivers with live tracking, add map services. Set aside 15% to 20% of the build cost each year for maintenance, because Apple and Google update their operating systems every year.

Two items are often forgotten. The first is food photography: a good photo of each item lifts orders more than any technical feature. The second is marketing the app itself: posters in the branches, a QR code on the receipt and the packaging, and an offer on the first order. An app your customers do not know about will not be downloaded, however good it is.

How to compare two quotes, and what to prepare

Ask for a live test: a complete order from your phone until it appears on the branch panel, with an item that has modifiers and choices. Then compare the items in writing. At AETHER we start with a written scope and a contract, payments tied to delivery, a written delivery date that we keep, and fast technical support after launch. The code and accounts are yours, and we send a written proposal within 24 hours.

  • Is the quote custom development or a copy of a ready-made solution? Ask for the answer in writing.
  • Does it include iOS, Android, the branch panel, the admin panel and the backend?
  • Is POS integration included in the price, and with which system exactly?
  • Who owns the store accounts and the customer data?
  • What does support look like in the evening and at weekends?
  • Prepare before you ask: the menu with its modifiers and prices, number of branches and delivery zones, your current POS system, how you deliver, and payment methods.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an app for my restaurant cost?

A ready-made app under your brand costs EGP 67,500–375,000 in the market or a monthly subscription, a custom first version EGP 180,000–450,000, and a chain app on iOS and Android with an admin panel and payments EGP 450,000–1,050,000. With us a custom app starts from EGP 180,000; the real figure is set once we know the number of branches, how you deliver and which POS system you use.

How long does it take to build a restaurant app?

A ready-made app launches in days to weeks. A custom first version usually takes 6 to 12 weeks, and a chain app with POS and delivery integration 3 to 5 months. Add a few days for Apple and Google review before publishing. What delays launch most is preparing the menu with its photos and modifiers, so start on it early.

Does my own app replace the delivery platforms?

Usually not at first. Delivery platforms bring customers who do not know you; your app keeps the customer who does. The practical plan is to stay on the platforms for new customers and move repeat customers to your app with a first-order offer and a QR code on the packaging. Over time the share of direct orders, on which you pay no commission, goes up.

Does the app connect to my POS system?

It does if your POS system offers an API. The order then goes straight into the system, prints in the kitchen and counts in sales. If the system does not allow it, the branch receives orders on a separate screen. Ask your POS vendor before you request quotes, and name the system in your request so the integration is priced as a clear line item.

Do I need my own delivery drivers?

Not necessarily. You can start with branch pickup only, or connect the app to a third-party delivery company that collects and delivers the order. Your own drivers give you more control over the experience, but they need a driver app, tracking and daily management, and they raise both build and running costs. Decide based on your daily delivery order volume.

What is the difference between a subscription app and a custom app?

A ready-made app is quicker and cheaper at the start, but you pay a permanent subscription and are limited to its features, and your customer data and store account may sit with the provider. A custom app is yours to own and develop, with a higher first cost. If you go ready-made, make sure you can export customer data and that the store accounts are in your business’s name.

Can I add loyalty points and offers?

Yes. Common setups for restaurants are points on every order, a free meal after a set number, or coupons for specific customers such as those who have not ordered for a month. A simple version can go into the first release, with tiers and a wallet left for later. What matters is that points also count for in-branch orders, which needs POS integration.

Does the app issue a tax invoice?

The business that sells to the customer is the one bound by the e-invoicing requirements of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority, according to its wave. The app can issue the invoice itself, or it can come from the connected POS system, which is usually simpler. Put this in the scope of work from the start, and ask your accountant about the treatment.

Do I own the code and my customer data?

Yes. At AETHER the code, accounts and data belong to the client, and this is written into the contract before work starts. The App Store, Google Play, payment gateway and server accounts are registered in your business’s name, and you can export customers and orders at any time. We commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after launch.
