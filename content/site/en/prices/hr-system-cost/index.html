When is ready-made software enough, and when do you need a custom system?

A small company with fixed hours and simple pay is well served by ready-made cloud software on a monthly subscription. It is live quickly, covers files, leave and payroll, and needs no upfront investment. Building a custom system for twenty employees on one shift is spending without a reason.

A custom system makes sense when the rules get complicated: rotating shifts, field sites, overtime with different formulas, allowances that change by project, day-rate labour, and several commercial registrations. That is when ready-made software starts forcing workarounds and part of the calculation goes back to Excel. And as headcount grows, a per-employee subscription becomes a yearly figure worth comparing with the cost of a system you own.

What drives the price of an HR system?

Most of the cost is in the calculation rules, not the screens. Every rule particular to your company needs analysis and testing:

  • The number of employees, branches and companies in the group.
  • Attendance: fingerprint devices, a mobile app with location, or import from existing devices.
  • Working-time rules: shifts, lateness and early leaving, overtime, Ramadan hours.
  • Leave: balances, carry-over, several leave types and an approval path.
  • Payroll: allowances, deductions, loans, instalments and disciplinary deductions.
  • End-of-service entitlements and settling leave when a contract ends.
  • Wage protection and bank transfer files, and calculating social insurance contributions.
  • Self-service for employees and managers on mobile.
  • Extra modules: recruitment, appraisals, training, assets in custody, warnings.

The first version: enough to close a correct payroll

The realistic goal of phase one is a correct payroll that comes out of the system without Excel. So it includes the employee file, contract and documents with alerts for expiring residence permits and licences; attendance; leave; loans and deductions; payroll; the wage protection file; and a payslip for the employee. This sits in the lower and middle part of the EGP 282,000–562,500 range.

Phase two adds self-service, the mobile app and approval paths. Phase three adds recruitment, appraisals and training. The order is deliberate: appraisal and training modules succeed only once employees and management trust the attendance and pay figures. Starting with them is one of the most common reasons HR projects stall.

Saudi requirements in payroll systems

A payroll system in Egypt deals with official bodies and platforms: wage protection through the Mudad platform, contract documentation on Qiwa, social insurance contributions, and end-of-service entitlements under the Labour Law. A good system prepares the payroll file in the required format, calculates contributions on the contributory wage, and flags missing data before upload so the file is not rejected.

The details of these requirements change, and applying them to your case is the job of your HR specialist or adviser; we do not give legal advice. What matters technically is that calculation rules can be changed in the settings rather than written into the code, so a change in a policy or a rate does not need a programmer. Ask about any automated connection available with these platforms and the approval it needs, and put it in the scope of work.

Hidden costs in HR projects

The first is data migration: leave balances, outstanding loans, start dates, and wages with their history. This data is usually scattered across files and people, and cleaning it takes more of your team’s time than the programmer’s. The second is attendance hardware: if you have fingerprint devices, check that they can be integrated; otherwise budget for replacing them or relying on mobile.

The third is parallel running: it is wise to run the new system alongside the old method for a month or two and reconcile the two payrolls before full adoption, which is a temporary doubling of effort. The fourth is yearly support of 15% to 25% of the implementation cost, which includes changing rules when policies change. Employee and salary data is sensitive personal data under the Personal Data Protection Law, so ask about permissions, encryption and the access log.

How to compare two HR system quotes

Give each provider three real cases from your company, such as an employee on a night shift with overtime, an employee whose contract has ended with a leave balance, and an employee repaying a loan in instalments, and ask them to calculate each in front of you. Then compare:

  • Are shift and allowance rules set in the settings, or do they need programming every time they change?
  • Are the wage protection file, social insurance calculation and end of service included?
  • Are data migration and training inside the price, and for how many days?
  • The total cost over three years at the headcount you expect.
  • Does it connect to your current attendance devices and accounting system?
  • Who can see salaries? Ask to see the permissions and the access log.
  • Who owns the code and data, and can it be exported at any time?

Frequently asked questions

How much does an HR and payroll system cost for a mid-sized company?

With us an HR system starts from EGP 282,000, and a custom system that covers employee files, attendance, leave and payroll usually costs EGP 282,000–562,500. As a module inside an ERP alongside accounting, and for multi-company groups, it is scoped after a free analysis session. The real price depends on how complex your shift and allowance rules are, more than on headcount.

Is ready-made cloud software enough for me?

If your hours are fixed, your pay is simple and your headcount is limited, usually yes, and it is the cheaper route. Test it with your hard cases before you subscribe: a night shift, overtime, a loan in instalments, end of service. If it calculates them correctly without workarounds, it fits. If you go back to Excel for every payroll, it is time for a custom system.

How long does it take to implement an HR system?

A custom system usually takes 8 to 14 weeks for phase one, covering rule analysis, programming, data migration and training. We recommend a month or two of parallel running to reconcile the new payroll with the old before full adoption. What delays the project most is collecting and checking leave balances and loans.

Does the system prepare the wage protection file?

Yes. Once the payroll is approved, the system produces the payroll file in the format required for upload to the Mudad platform, along with the bank transfer file and a report of missing data, such as an incomplete ID number or bank account. That reduces rejected files after upload. Applying the requirements to your case remains the job of your HR specialist.

Does the system calculate end of service and overtime?

Yes, according to rules set in the settings in line with the Labour Law and your company policy. The system calculates overtime from the attendance record using the defined formula, and works out end-of-service entitlements from length of service and the reason the contract ended, settling leave balance and loans. Review the rules with your adviser before you approve them.

Can employees record attendance from their phones?

Yes, attendance can be recorded from a mobile app with a location check, which suits field teams and project sites with no fingerprint device. The two methods can be combined: fingerprint in the office and mobile on site. Mention your current attendance devices in your request so we can confirm they can be integrated.

What is the difference between a standalone HR system and an ERP module?

Standalone is faster and cheaper if your problem is only payroll and attendance, and it can be linked to accounting with a monthly entry. A module inside an ERP posts payroll to accounting automatically and ties labour cost to projects and cost centres. If you plan an ERP soon, build HR inside it from the start to avoid a second migration.

Who can see salaries in the system?

Only the people you decide. Permissions are built so an employee sees their own payslip, a manager sees their team’s attendance and leave without their pay if you wish, and the payroll officer sees the full payroll. Every view and change is recorded in the activity log. Employee salaries are sensitive personal data under the Personal Data Protection Law.

Do the code and data belong to my company?

Yes. At AETHER the code, data and accounts belong to the client, this is written into the contract before work starts, and we sign an NDA on request. You can export employee data and payrolls at any time, and you pay no yearly licence per employee. We commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after go-live.
