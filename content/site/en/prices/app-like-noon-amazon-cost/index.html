Online store versus multi-vendor marketplace

Noon and Amazon are well-known online marketplaces where many sellers list products in one storefront and a customer buys from several sellers in one basket. Both names are used here for illustration only; we have no connection with either company. The difference between this model and an ordinary store is not in what the customer sees, but in everything that happens after the buy button.

An ordinary store has one seller, one stock and one invoice. In a marketplace a single order splits into shipments from different sellers, each seller has their own stock, prices and returns policy, and the platform collects the money, takes its commission and pays the rest to each seller. That logic is what lifts the cost from store ranges to platform ranges, and it is the first thing to ask about in any quote.

What drives the price of a marketplace?

Every item below is a business decision before it is a technical one. Settling it early saves weeks of rework:

  • Seller onboarding: open to everyone, or with document review and a contract?
  • Seller dashboard: adding products, bulk upload from a file, stock, orders, statements.
  • Commissions: one rate, or rates by category or seller, plus subscription or listing fees.
  • Order splitting and shipping: who ships, the seller or the platform, and how are shipping fees calculated?
  • Returns, refunds and disputes between customer and seller.
  • Settlements: when is the seller paid, and how are returns deducted?
  • Search, filtering and comparison across thousands of products.
  • Payments: mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay, Tabby, Tamara and cash on delivery.
  • E-invoicing and VAT: who issues the invoice to the customer?

The first version: start with one category

Marketplaces that succeed start narrow: one category the founder knows well, such as car parts, restaurant supplies, home-business products or building materials. With one category you need only a simple taxonomy and a limited number of sellers you know personally, and you can handle returns and disputes by hand from the admin panel.

A first web version with seller sign-up, a seller dashboard, a catalogue, cart, payment and an admin panel starts with us from EGP 282,000 and usually costs EGP 282,000–562,500. Postpone the mobile apps until you see customers coming back, and postpone platform-run fulfilment, paid seller advertising and wallets. A fast responsive site serves most buyers at the start, and a mobile app pays off once purchases repeat.

Ready-made platform or custom build?

There are add-ons that turn ready-made stores into multi-vendor marketplaces, and they suit testing the idea with a handful of sellers. Their limits show in complex settlements, split shipping, performance with thousands of products, and any logic particular to your sector. Changing them deeply can cost more than building anew.

Custom development costs more at the start, but you own the code and build the commission, shipping and settlement rules your business needs. A practical rule: if what sets your marketplace apart is only the products and the marketing, a ready-made platform may be enough. If it is the way of working itself, such as wholesale to businesses, per-customer pricing or rental, custom development is the realistic route.

Payments, settlements and invoicing

The most neglected part of marketplace quotes is the money after payment. The platform receives the full order amount and must then know exactly what each seller is owed after commission, shipping, discounts and returns. Ask to see the seller statement and the settlement report in the demo, not just the product page.

Card details are processed by the licensed payment gateway and are not stored in your system. Collecting money on behalf of sellers and paying it out to them may carry regulatory requirements, so discuss the settlement method with the payment gateway and your adviser before you fix the model. And decide who issues the tax invoice to the customer, the seller or the platform, because that changes the whole e-invoicing logic.

Hidden costs and running costs

A marketplace grows in data quickly: images for thousands of products, search, and traffic peaks in seasons and sales. That means scalable cloud hosting, not shared hosting. Add shipping integrations, which start in the market at EGP 7,500 each, verification and notification messages, and payment gateway fees on every transaction.

Set aside 15% to 20% of the build cost a year for maintenance, or a monthly support plan that starts in the market at EGP 22,500. But the biggest running cost is not technical: a team that reviews sellers’ products, answers customers, resolves disputes, and attracts sellers and buyers at the same time. Cost it before you set the development budget.

How to compare two quotes, and what to prepare

Ask each provider to show you one order from two different sellers, from purchase through to settlement. Then compare the items below. At AETHER we send a written proposal within 24 hours that breaks down every part, with a written scope and contract, payments tied to delivery, a delivery date we keep, and fast technical support after launch.

  • Does the quote include the seller dashboard, the admin panel, and the commission and settlement logic?
  • How is an order split and shipping calculated when there are several sellers?
  • How are returns handled in the seller’s account?
  • Are the code and data yours, and can seller and product data be exported?
  • Has performance been tested with the number of products you expect?
  • Prepare before you ask: your starting category, the expected number of sellers, the revenue model, who ships, and the payment methods.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a site like Noon or Amazon cost?

With us a multi-vendor marketplace starts from EGP 282,000: a first web version usually costs EGP 282,000–562,500, while a full marketplace with commissions, settlements and shipping, mobile apps and large scale are scoped after a free analysis session. Nobody builds the scale of the major platforms in a first version; the aim is a marketplace that works on the same logic in a defined category.

How long does it take to build a multi-vendor marketplace?

A first web version usually takes 3 to 5 months, and longer with mobile apps, complex settlements and several shipping integrations. You can shorten it by launching in phases: catalogue, ordering and payment first, then automated settlements and advanced reports. Onboarding sellers and their products takes time, so start it in parallel with development.

Should I start with a website only, or do I need a mobile app?

Start with a fast responsive website that works well on mobile. Customers buy without downloading anything, it appears in Google results, and it costs clearly less. An app pays off when you have repeat customers and want notifications and personalised offers. If the backend is built with APIs from the start, adding an app later does not mean rebuilding.

How does the platform make money from sellers?

The common models are a commission on each sale, a monthly seller subscription, listing and promotion fees, or service fees such as storage and shipping. Each model changes what is built: commissions need accurate settlements, and subscriptions need plans and billing. Decide your model before asking for a quote, because it is one of the things that moves the price most.

Who ships the order: the seller or the platform?

Both are possible. Seller shipping is simpler and cheaper to build, but the customer experience varies from one seller to another. Platform shipping through integrated carriers gives one consistent experience and one tracking flow, and needs integration, shipping pricing and returns management. Many marketplaces start with seller shipping and move gradually to unified shipping.

Does the marketplace support mada, Tabby and Tamara?

Yes, a marketplace can be connected to mada, Apple Pay, STC Pay, Tabby, Tamara and cash on delivery. Each gateway needs a merchant account and approval from its provider, so start the paperwork early. For marketplaces in particular, discuss with the payment gateway how amounts are distributed to sellers, and calculate commissions after gateway fees.

What is the difference between a multi-vendor add-on and a custom build?

A ready-made add-on is quick and cheap, and suits testing the idea with a limited number of sellers. A custom build shapes commission, shipping and settlement rules around the way you work, copes with larger product volumes, and you own its code. If your plan is a marketplace that grows for years, treat the add-on as a first trial and make sure you can export your data from it.

Does a marketplace need e-invoicing integration?

The business that issues the tax invoice to the customer must meet the e-invoicing requirements of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA) according to its wave. In a marketplace you first have to decide who issues the invoice, the seller or the platform, and how the platform invoices its commission to the seller. Put this in the scope from the start, and ask your accountant about the tax treatment.

Do I own the code and the seller data?

At AETHER the code, accounts and data belong to the client, and this is written into the contract. Servers, payment gateways and store accounts are registered in your company’s name, and you can export seller, product and order data at any time. We commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after launch.
