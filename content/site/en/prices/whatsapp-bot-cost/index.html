Three kinds of WhatsApp bot, at three price points

The first kind is a menu bot: “Press 1 for prices, 2 for branches.” It is built on a subscription platform in days, it is cheap, and it is enough if your customers’ questions are limited and repetitive. Its weakness is that a customer who types their question in their own words gets no answer, so they move to a staff member or leave.

The second kind is an AI bot that understands Arabic and the Gulf dialect and answers from your company’s information: prices, services and policies. The third is an agent that works on your behalf: it books an appointment in your system, sends a quote, checks an order’s status, logs the customer in your CRM and hands them to a sales rep. The price difference between the three reflects the difference in the work the bot actually does.

What drives the price of a WhatsApp bot?

These are the elements that move the cost in WhatsApp projects; the clearer you are about them in your request, the more accurate the quote:

  • Type of bot: fixed menus, or AI that understands free-text questions.
  • How many tasks it performs: answering, booking, quoting, order tracking, collections.
  • Integration with your systems: CRM, ERP, booking system or online store.
  • Quality of its Arabic and dialect, and whether it understands voice notes.
  • Handover to a human with the full conversation context.
  • Monthly message volume and the number of numbers and branches.
  • Reports: customers’ most frequent questions and conversion rates.

Recurring costs you need to budget for

The official WhatsApp Business API charges for messages the business starts: in Saudi Arabia, about EGP 2.85 for a marketing message and about EGP 0.6 for a utility message or verification code, according to prices published by service providers. Replies within a conversation the customer started are treated differently, so the bill depends on how you use it.

An AI bot adds the cost of the language model per conversation, hosting, and ongoing monitoring to improve its answers. Market estimates put running costs for integrated automation at EGP 45,000–120,000 a month. Compare that with the salary of a customer service employee who works limited hours, while the bot replies around the clock.

Common mistakes that raise the cost or sink the project

The most common is using a personal number or the regular WhatsApp app with unofficial tools; the number gets banned after a bulk send and the conversation history is lost. Use the official WhatsApp Business API from the start, and ramp up sending gradually from a new number.

The second mistake is letting the bot give wrong prices or commitments because it was not given accurate information or clear limits. A good bot knows when to say “let me pass you to a colleague” and hands the conversation over with a summary. The third is launching without reviewing its conversations in the first weeks to improve its answers.

How to compare two WhatsApp bot quotes

Ask for a live test on real questions from your customers, in Arabic and in dialect, then compare:

  • Does it run on the official WhatsApp Business API?
  • Does it understand free-text questions, or menus only?
  • Which tasks does it actually perform in your systems, not just promise?
  • Who pays Meta’s fees and the AI model costs, and how are they estimated monthly?
  • Are the conversations and data yours, and stored in line with the PDPL?
  • How does it hand over to a human, and does the human see the full context?

How to start for less

Start with the ten questions you get most every day and one task that eats your team’s time, such as booking an appointment or sending a quote. Launch the bot on those, review its conversations weekly, then add tasks gradually. This is cheaper and more successful than building a bot that tries to do everything from day one.

When does a bot pay for itself?

Work out the return with three questions. First: how many messages do you get a day, and how many are repeat questions about prices, hours and branches? Second: how many leads are lost because the reply came late, after hours or at the weekend? Third: how many hours does your team spend copying and pasting the same answers?

If the bot will answer half of the repeat messages, catch the customers who message at night, and book or quote them before they go to a competitor, the return usually shows within a few months. If your messages are few and every conversation involves its own negotiation, an organised shared inbox on a simple subscription platform may be enough to start with.

What to prepare before building the bot

A bot is only as good as the information it is built on. Prepare these before you start to shorten the timeline and get accurate answers from day one:

  • A list of the most frequent questions with approved answers.
  • Up-to-date prices, services, branches and opening hours.
  • The cases the bot must hand to a staff member immediately.
  • A dedicated company WhatsApp number and a verified business account.
  • The systems it will work with: booking, quotations, CRM.
  • The right tone for your brand: formal or friendly.

Ready-made platform or custom agent?

Ready-made WhatsApp platforms are excellent for organising a shared inbox across several staff, sending campaigns and menu-based replies, at a low cost. But they are generic by nature, and know nothing about your quotation, booking or inventory system without extra integrations.

A custom agent is built around your business: it reads prices from your system, books into your calendar, logs the customer in your CRM, and knows when to hand over the conversation and to whom. It costs more upfront, but it does work that used to need an employee. Many companies start with a ready-made platform and move to an agent once they know which tasks consume their team’s time.

How to measure the bot’s success after launch

Watch four numbers in the first month: the share of conversations the bot completed without staff, the average first response time, the number of bookings or quotes it created, and the conversations handed to staff and why. The last one is your guide to improving the bot, because it reveals the questions it has not yet been trained on.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a WhatsApp bot cost for a business?

Ready-made reply platforms cost about EGP 480–8,235 a month. With us a bot starts from EGP 37,500: a basic AI chatbot costs EGP 37,500–120,000, and automation integrated with your systems EGP 120,000–375,000 to set up, with running costs of EGP 45,000–120,000 a month. The difference between them is the work the bot actually does, not what it is called.

Does the bot understand the Saudi dialect?

Modern AI bots understand Modern Standard Arabic and Gulf dialects well, provided they are tested on real messages from your customers and their answers are tuned. Menu bots do not understand free text at all, so ask about the type of bot before the price. Ask for a test on twenty real messages from your customers before you sign.

How long does it take to set up a WhatsApp bot?

A menu bot on a ready-made platform is set up in days. A smart bot that answers from your company’s information takes two to four weeks, and an agent connected to your systems 4 to 8 weeks. Add time for Meta to approve your WhatsApp Business account. Start the verification early, because it can take several days.

What does Meta charge for WhatsApp messages?

In Saudi Arabia, messages started by the business are charged by type: about EGP 2.85 for a marketing message and about EGP 0.6 for utility messages and verification codes, according to prices published by service providers. Prices can change, so check them when you sign. Replies within a conversation started by the customer are treated differently, so estimate based on how you use it.

Can my number get banned?

Yes, if you use the regular WhatsApp app with unofficial tools, or send a large bulk message from a new number all at once. Use the official WhatsApp Business API, increase sending volume gradually, and only message people who have agreed to be contacted. Avoid buying number lists; they are the fastest route to a ban and damage your brand’s reputation.

Can the bot book appointments and send quotes?

Yes, if it is connected to your booking or quotation system. It asks the customer for the details, checks available times, confirms the booking and sends the confirmation and reminder, or prepares and sends a quote. That integration is what raises the cost above a simple bot. The result is fewer calls, fewer missed appointments and replies around the clock.

Can the bot replace a customer service employee?

It takes over a large share of repetitive work and replies around the clock, but it does not remove the need for staff in special cases, complaints and large deals. The best results come from a bot that answers and books, then hands the conversation to a staff member with a summary when needed. That way your team focuses on the most important customers instead of repeating the same answers.

Are customer conversations stored securely?

The conversations and data should be yours, stored with clear permissions in line with the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL). Ask where they are stored, who can see them, and whether they can be exported. Hosting in the region is possible on request. We sign an NDA if you wish, and the conversations and data stay yours in every case, exportable whenever you like.

Does the bot reply to voice notes?

AI bots can: the voice note is transcribed to text and then answered, which matters because many customers in Egypt prefer voice notes. Accuracy depends on audio clarity and dialect, so test it on real messages before relying on it in important conversations.

What is the difference between a WhatsApp bot and a website chatbot?

The idea is the same but the channel differs. A WhatsApp bot reaches customers in the app they use every day and can send reminders and follow-ups later, while a website chatbot only serves visitors while they browse. Both can be built on the same knowledge so the answers match across the two channels.
