What drives the price of a corporate website?

A corporate site is not a store or a campaign page: its visitor is looking for a reason to trust you, not a buy button. The procurement officer opens it before the meeting, the job applicant before the interview, the bank or partner before signing. So it is measured by what it proves about the company, and that is what moves the price:

  • Service and sector pages: one page per service and per sector you serve, not one page for everything.
  • Languages: Arabic only, or Arabic and English with content written for each.
  • Content panel: can your own staff add a project, a news item or a vacancy without a developer?
  • Projects and track record: photos, descriptions and filtering by sector.
  • Integrations: CRM, WhatsApp, email, quotation request forms, a careers portal.
  • Content: who writes the Arabic and English copy, and who takes the photos.
  • SEO: pages built around what your customers really search for.
  • Security and hosting requirements, especially if you deal with large organisations.

First version versus the full site

A first version that does the job: a home page that says what the company does and for whom, a page for each main service, an “About” page with real people, a projects section with what you are allowed to publish, a contact page with a form and a WhatsApp button, and a content panel. This usually costs EGP 84,000–168,750 depending on page count and design depth.

What can wait: a large blog, a download centre, a client portal and detailed sector pages. What we do not recommend postponing: building the site to support a second language even if it launches in Arabic only, and making projects and services content you manage from the panel rather than pages a developer writes. The first makes adding English later a content job, not a rebuild. The second keeps the site alive after handover.

A bilingual site: where the cost goes

In the market a bilingual company site usually costs EGP 90,000–180,000. The extra cost is not the language switch button. It is three things: a design that works right-to-left and left-to-right without breaking, English content written for its reader rather than translated word for word, and separate SEO for each language with its own titles and URLs.

Before you ask for both languages, ask who reads the English version. If your customers are foreign companies, investors or procurement teams in multinationals, it is essential from day one. If your audience is local, launch in Arabic on a structure that supports both, and add English when you need it.

The content panel and integrations: what makes the site work

A site without a content panel stops being updated within months, because every change needs a developer and an invoice. Ask to see the panel before you sign: add a project with photos, edit a service description, publish a news item in both languages. If you need a long explanation to do that, your staff will not use it.

Integrations are what turn the site from a brochure into a sales channel. The minimum: every form reaches the responsible person at once, with the traffic source. Better: the request enters the CRM, is assigned automatically, and triggers a reminder if nobody replies. An automated assistant can also answer visitors and record their requests out of hours. These additions move the site into the integrations range, and it is scoped after a free analysis session.

Template or custom design? And the yearly costs

A ready-made template, at EGP 52,500–105,000 in the market, gives you a site within days. It suits a new company that wants a quick presence. Its limits for established companies: a look shared with many other sites, sections that do not match how you present your services, extra code that slows pages down, and difficulty connecting to your systems. A custom design is built around your company’s message and audience, and it is faster and easier to extend.

After launch, budget for the domain at EGP 750–2,250 a year, hosting and an SSL certificate at EGP 4,500–60,000, and maintenance at EGP 15,000–75,000 a year for a small site. Add company email, font and image licences, and updating the content itself, which is ongoing work for your team rather than the developer.

Common mistakes on corporate websites

The first is registering the domain and hosting in the agency’s name, or in the name of an employee who has since left. Nothing holds companies up more when they change supplier, and a clause in the contract settles it. The second is a good-looking site with copy that could belong to any company: “we provide integrated solutions of the highest quality”. The visitor wants to know exactly what you do, for whom, and what the evidence is.

The third is publishing client names, logos or project details without written permission. The fourth is forms that collect visitor data with no clear privacy policy; the details of enquirers and job applicants are personal data under the Personal Data Protection Law. The fifth is launching the site and forgetting it: a news page whose latest item is two years old does more harm than no news page.

How to compare two quotes, and what to prepare

Put both quotes in one table and compare what is delivered, not the final number. At AETHER we commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after launch. The code, accounts and domain are yours, and we send a written proposal within 24 hours.

  • Are the page count and the content of each page written into the quote, for both languages?
  • Who writes the Arabic and English content, and who supplies the photos?
  • Can you manage services, projects, news and vacancies from the content panel?
  • Where does each form go, and is the traffic source recorded?
  • Are the domain, hosting and Google accounts in your company’s name?
  • Prepare before you ask: your list of services and sectors, the projects you may publish, your visual identity, the languages, the systems the site will connect to, and who will update content after launch.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a corporate website cost in Egypt?

A professional company site with custom design and a content panel usually costs EGP 84,000–168,750 in the market, a bilingual site EGP 90,000–180,000, and an advanced site connected to a CRM and WhatsApp is scoped after a free analysis session. With us a company website starts from EGP 84,000; the real figure is set once we know the page count, languages, integrations and who writes the content.

How long does it take to build a company website?

A typical company site takes 4 to 8 weeks, a bilingual site or one with integrations 6 to 12 weeks, and enterprise sites longer. What delays delivery most is late content and internal approvals, so we recommend naming one person on your side who owns sign-off, and preparing copy and photos before design starts.

How is a corporate website different from an ordinary site?

A corporate site exists to build trust and collect serious requests from companies and public bodies, not to sell directly. So it centres on service and sector pages, track record, certificates and the team, with quotation request forms that reach sales. It usually needs two languages, a content panel and a link to the sales system, which is what makes it cost more than a few-page site.

Do I need the site in two languages?

You do if some of your customers or partners read English: foreign companies, investors, procurement teams in multinationals. If your audience is local, Arabic is enough to start, provided the site is built to support a second language. In the market a bilingual site costs EGP 90,000–180,000.

Can I edit the site content myself?

Yes, if the quote includes a content panel that manages services, projects, news and vacancies in both languages. Ask to try it before you sign, and for training for your staff at handover. Some cheap quotes deliver fixed pages written by a developer, so every change becomes a paid request. Ask about this directly.

Does the site connect to a CRM and WhatsApp?

Yes. Every request can enter the CRM with the traffic source and the page it came from, be assigned to a person and trigger an alert. A WhatsApp button opens a chat with a ready message naming the service. If you already have a sales system, name it in your request so the integration is priced as a clear line item.

Does the price include copywriting and photography?

In most quotes, no. Many suppliers put in placeholder text and wait for your content, and professional photography is always a separate item. Ask for Arabic and English copywriting to be a clear line with its own price. Content is what convinces the visitor and what gets the site found on Google, so do not leave it to the end.

What does the site cost each year after launch?

The domain costs EGP 750–2,250 a year, hosting and an SSL certificate EGP 4,500–60,000 depending on the size of the site, and maintenance for a small site EGP 15,000–75,000 a year. Large sites need a monthly support plan, which costs EGP 22,500–180,000 in the market. Always ask for the renewal price in year two.

Do the site and the domain belong to my company?

Yes. At AETHER the code, accounts and domain belong to the client, and this is written into the contract before work starts. The domain, hosting and Google accounts are registered in your company’s name, and the full source code is handed over. We commit to a written delivery date, with fast technical support after launch.
